Ten games for you

Lumi grew up and is now in third grade. Here are ten math games: multiplication, division, fractions, measurement and shapes. You can listen to every instruction and answer by touching. You can play all by yourself!

For the teacher and the family

The ten modules cover the Illinois third grade mathematics standards (Illinois Learning Standards: 3.OA, 3.NBT, 3.NF, 3.MD and 3.G). They can be used in any order, although the list follows the usual sequence of the year: first the meaning of multiplication and division, then fractions, measurement and shapes.

What changes in third grade

In second grade the games are more guided and concrete; in third grade they ask for more strategy and independence, without losing the fun. It is not a rigid line: the challenge should fit what each child can do, not only their grade.

  • Multi-step instructions that stay on screen so the child can check them independently.
  • Plan before calculating: at the Challenge level, word problems ask for the operation first and the answer second.
  • Connect clues and compare options: the Number detective chains clues to rule out cards, and Sharing with a rule asks the child to mark and check every option that works.
  • Explain decisions: after a correct answer Lumi retells the path (“after clue 1, 3 were left…”); at the end of each game it asks “Which strategy did you use? What would you change?” and comments on the choice without grading it.
  • For collaboration: play in pairs, one person reads the clues and the other decides, then they check the result together.

Reference: National Association for the Education of Young Children (naeyc.org), on play in the early grades.

How they are designed

  • Every instruction is spoken, and the speaker button repeats it as often as needed; word problems are read aloud too.
  • Meaning first, memory second: equal groups, arrays and number-line jumps show what multiplication and division mean before practicing the facts.
  • Mistakes are not penalized: a gentle tone, a hint and another try. When the mistake is a typical one, such as mixing up 4 groups of 6 with 6 groups of 4, the hint explains it.
  • Each game has two levels, Easy and Challenge, so the same page can stay with the child all year.
  • Each game has its own direct link with an anchor (#), to send the child straight to the game of the day.
  • Everything works with a keyboard and a screen reader, respects reduced motion and stores no child data. Each module includes a printable page.
