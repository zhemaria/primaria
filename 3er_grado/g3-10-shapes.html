Four-sided shapes and equal parts!

Lumi and Génesis investigate shapes: which ones are quadrilaterals, what are they called and what do they have in common? With chained clues they find the hidden shape and split shapes into equal parts.

Aligned to the Illinois third grade mathematics standards 3.G.1 (understand that shapes in different categories, such as rhombuses, rectangles and others, may share attributes, such as having four sides, and that the shared attributes can define a larger category, quadrilaterals; recognize rhombuses, rectangles and squares as examples of quadrilaterals and draw quadrilaterals that do not belong to any of these subcategories) and 3.G.2 (partition shapes into parts with equal areas and express the area of each part as a unit fraction of the whole). At the Easy level the sides are numbered, the attributes are written out and the grid is shown; at the Challenge level those supports are removed. Remember that a square is also a rectangle and a rhombus. Each game has its own direct link: #cuadrilatero, #nombre, #atributos, #clasifica, #detective and #partes.
