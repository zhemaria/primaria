Part 1 · Explain

Elías builds a box with unit cubes: each layer has 4 rows of 3 cubes, and there are 2 layers. He says the volume is 24 cubic units. Explain why he is right, using the layers.

Elías construye una caja con cubos unitarios: cada capa tiene 4 filas de 3 cubos y hay 2 capas. Dice que el volumen es 24 unidades cúbicas. Explica por qué tiene razón usando las capas.

Sentence starters: One layer has ___ × ___ = ___ cubes. There are ___ layers, so the volume is ___ × ___ = ___ cubic units. This is the same as V = l × w × h = ___.

Useful words: unit cube, layer, base, height, cubic units, volume.

Challenge: also explain why V = B × h gives the same answer.

Reto: explica también por qué V = B × h da la misma respuesta.

Part 2 · Apply the idea to a new problem

A planter is made of two boxes that do not overlap. Box A is 5 ft × 2 ft × 2 ft, and Box B is 3 ft × 2 ft × 1 ft. Selena says the volume is 5 × 2 × 2 × 3 × 2 × 1 = 120 cubic feet. Is she right? Explain, and find the correct volume.

Una jardinera está formada por dos cajas que no se superponen. La caja A mide 5 pies × 2 pies × 2 pies y la caja B, 3 pies × 2 pies × 1 pie. Selena dice que el volumen es 5 × 2 × 2 × 3 × 2 × 1 = 120 pies cúbicos. ¿Tiene razón? Explica y halla el volumen correcto.

Sentence starters: Selena is ___. Box A = ___ ft³ and Box B = ___ ft³. Because the boxes do not overlap, I ___ the volumes: ___ + ___ = ___ ft³.

Challenge: also explain why the boxes must not overlap for this method to work.

Reto: explica también por qué las cajas no deben superponerse para que este método funcione.

Check your own work

Read your answers again. Press each sentence that is true about your work.

Vuelve a leer tus respuestas. Pulsa cada oración que sea verdadera sobre tu trabajo.

Part 1. One layer has 4 rows of 3 cubes, so 4 × 3 = 12 cubes. There are 2 layers, so 12 × 2 = 24 cubes. The volume is 24 cubic units. With the formula, V = 4 × 3 × 2 = 24.

Part 2. Selena is not right. She multiplied all six numbers together, but each box has its own volume. Box A: 5 × 2 × 2 = 20 ft³. Box B: 3 × 2 × 1 = 6 ft³. The boxes do not overlap, so I add them: 20 + 6 = 26 ft³.

Challenge. B is the area of the base: 4 × 3 = 12 square units, and h = 2, so V = 12 × 2 = 24. If the boxes overlapped, the shared space would be counted twice, and the sum would be too big.

Aligned to the Illinois fifth grade mathematics standards 5.MD.1 (convert among different-sized standard measurement units within a given measurement system, e.g., convert 5 cm to 0.05 m, and use these conversions in solving multi-step, real world problems), 5.MD.2 (make a line plot to display a data set of measurements in fractions of a unit (1/2, 1/4, 1/8); use operations on fractions for this grade to solve problems involving information presented in line plots), 5.MD.3 (recognize volume as an attribute of solid figures and understand concepts of volume measurement: a cube with side length 1 unit is a “unit cube”, and a solid figure which can be packed without gaps or overlaps using n unit cubes has a volume of n cubic units), 5.MD.4 (measure volumes by counting unit cubes, using cubic cm, cubic in, cubic ft, and improvised units) and 5.MD.5 (relate volume to the operations of multiplication and addition: find the volume of a right rectangular prism with whole-number side lengths by packing it with unit cubes, apply the formulas V = l × w × h and V = b × h, and find volumes of solid figures composed of two non-overlapping right rectangular prisms by adding the volumes of the non-overlapping parts). At the Easy level a conversion table, labeled line plots, a box split into layers and drawings with every dimension show the structure; at the Challenge level there are multi-step problems, eighths, missing dimensions, square-versus-cubic units and figures where one length must be found first, and in “Who is right?” the child must also choose the reason.
