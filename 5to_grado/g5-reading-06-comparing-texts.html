Points of view and comparing texts

⚖️📰📔🗺️

Two texts, two points of view!

The same event can look very different to a reporter, a fan, a player or a crossing guard. With Lumi you will compare two accounts of the same event, put together facts from several texts, compare stories of the same genre, find out what a map, a photo or an illustration adds, tell facts from opinions and decide who is right. Quote the text to prove it! With Israel, Daniel, Elías, Sophia, Athena, Salma and Selena.

El mismo suceso puede verse muy distinto para un reportero, un aficionado, un jugador o una guardia de cruce. Con Lumi vas a comparar dos relatos del mismo suceso, juntar datos de varios textos, comparar cuentos del mismo género, descubrir qué aporta un mapa, una foto o una ilustración, distinguir hechos de opiniones y decidir quién tiene razón. ¡Cita el texto para demostrarlo!

Aligned to the Illinois fifth grade English Language Arts standards RI.5.6 (analyze multiple accounts of the same event or topic, noting important similarities and differences in the point of view they represent), RI.5.9 (integrate information from several texts on the same topic in order to write or speak about the subject knowledgeably), RL.5.9 (compare and contrast stories in the same genre, such as mysteries and adventure stories, on their approaches to similar themes and topics), RL.5.7 (analyze how visual and multimedia elements contribute to the meaning, tone or beauty of a text) and W.5.1b (provide logically ordered reasons that are supported by facts and details), together with RI.5.1 and RL.5.1 (quote accurately from a text when explaining what it says). Lumi can read the texts aloud, highlighting each sentence. At the Easy level the texts are shorter, the questions ask what is the same, what is different, which text tells a fact, which sentence is a fact or an opinion and what a visual shows, and after a mistake the sentence with the answer is marked in the text; at the Challenge level the texts are longer, the questions ask why the accounts differ (point of view, purpose, what each writer could see or know), which statement combines information from two texts, how two stories approach the same theme, how a visual adds to meaning or tone, which reason is supported by facts and in what order the reasons go, and the child must prove the answer by picking the sentence that supports it. Every wrong answer comes from a typical mistake (a detail that only one text gives, two accounts switched, an opinion taken as a fact, a reason with no support), and its hint explains it. The people in the accounts and stories are made-up characters; the facts about Chicago, its history and nature are true. The optional “Ayuda en español” button translates only the instruction and the question; the texts, the options and the hints stay in English (WIDA-friendly support that keeps the reading level and the home-language help separate). Each game has its own direct link: #relatos, #integrar, #genero, #visuales, #hechoOpinion and #debate.

Alineado con los estándares de Lengua y Literatura de Illinois para quinto grado: RI.5.6 (analizar varios relatos del mismo suceso o tema y señalar semejanzas y diferencias importantes en el punto de vista que representan), RI.5.9 (integrar información de varios textos sobre el mismo tema para escribir o hablar del tema con conocimiento), RL.5.9 (comparar y contrastar cuentos del mismo género, como los de misterio y de aventuras, en su manera de tratar temas parecidos), RL.5.7 (analizar cómo los elementos visuales y multimedia contribuyen al significado, al tono o a la belleza de un texto) y W.5.1b (dar razones en un orden lógico, apoyadas en hechos y detalles). En el nivel Fácil los textos son más cortos y, tras un error, se marca la oración con la respuesta; en el Reto los textos son más largos y hay que demostrar la respuesta eligiendo la oración que la apoya. Desde quinto grado los juegos son en inglés; el botón «Ayuda en español» traduce solo la consigna y la pregunta.
