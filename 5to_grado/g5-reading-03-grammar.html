Write like an editor!

Good writers choose every word and every comma on purpose. With Lumi you will discover the job of conjunctions, prepositions and interjections, use the perfect tenses (had walked, have walked, will have walked), fix verbs that jump to the wrong time, complete pairs like either/or and neither/nor, place commas and mark titles, and combine short sentences into better ones. Israel, Daniel, Elías, Sophia, Athena, Salma and Selena need an editor. Are you ready?

Los buenos escritores escogen cada palabra y cada coma a propósito. Con Lumi vas a descubrir para qué sirven las conjunciones, las preposiciones y las interjecciones, usar los tiempos perfectos (had walked, have walked, will have walked), corregir verbos que saltan al tiempo equivocado, completar parejas como either/or y neither/nor, poner comas y marcar títulos, y unir oraciones cortas para escribir otras mejores.

Aligned to the Illinois fifth grade English Language Arts standards L.5.1a (explain the function of conjunctions, prepositions, and interjections in general and their function in particular sentences), L.5.1b (form and use the perfect verb tenses: I had walked, I have walked, I will have walked), L.5.1c (use verb tense to convey various times, sequences, states, and conditions), L.5.1d (recognize and correct inappropriate shifts in verb tense), L.5.1e (use correlative conjunctions such as either/or and neither/nor), L.5.2a (use punctuation to separate items in a series), L.5.2b (use a comma to separate an introductory element from the rest of the sentence), L.5.2c (use a comma to set off the words yes and no, to set off a tag question from the rest of the sentence, and to indicate direct address), L.5.2d (use underlining, quotation marks, or italics to indicate titles of works) and L.5.3a (expand, combine, and reduce sentences for meaning, reader/listener interest, and style). At the Easy level a key or a rule label is on the screen, the time line shows when each action happens, and in a paragraph the words that tell the time are marked after a mistake; at the Challenge level the keys are removed and the child must explain: what a word does in that particular sentence, which version fixes a tense shift (and which tense changes are really needed), irregular participles (had written, have gone, will have flown), subject–verb agreement with neither/nor, italics or quotation marks for titles, and why one combined sentence is better. Titles in italics on the screen are underlined when written by hand. Every wrong answer has its own hint. The optional “Ayuda en español” button translates only the instruction or the question (never the sentences, the answer options or the hints), a WIDA-friendly support; the game itself stays in English. Each game has its own direct link: #funciones, #perfectos, #cambios, #correlativas, #comas and #combinar.

Alineado con los estándares de lenguaje de Illinois para quinto grado: L.5.1a (explicar la función de las conjunciones, las preposiciones y las interjecciones en general y en oraciones concretas), L.5.1b (formar y usar los tiempos perfectos), L.5.1c (usar los tiempos verbales para expresar distintos momentos, secuencias, estados y condiciones), L.5.1d (reconocer y corregir cambios de tiempo verbal inapropiados), L.5.1e (usar conjunciones correlativas como either/or y neither/nor), L.5.2a a L.5.2d (comas en una serie, después de un elemento introductorio, con yes y no, con preguntas de confirmación y al dirigirse a alguien; subrayado, comillas o cursiva para los títulos) y L.5.3a (ampliar, combinar y reducir oraciones). Desde quinto grado los juegos son en inglés; el botón «Ayuda en español» traduce solo la consigna o la pregunta.
