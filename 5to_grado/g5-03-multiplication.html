Big numbers, smart steps!

Multiplying big numbers is easier when you take it one step at a time. With Lumi you will estimate first, break numbers into partial products with an area model, use the standard algorithm (without forgetting the placeholder zero!), solve word problems from school, find the patterns of zeros and decide who is right. With Israel, Daniel, Elías, Sophia, Athena, Salma and Selena.

Multiplicar números grandes es más fácil si se hace paso a paso. Part 1 · Explain

Selena multiplied 386 × 42 and got 16,212. Explain how you can know that her answer is reasonable without doing the whole problem again.

Selena multiplicó 386 × 42 y obtuvo 16,212. Explica cómo puedes saber que su respuesta es razonable sin volver a hacer todo el problema.

Sentence starters: I can round 386 to ___ and 42 to ___. Then ___ × ___ = ___. 16,212 is close to ___, so ___.

Part 2 · Apply the idea to a new problem

Israel multiplied 34 × 26 like this: 34 × 6 = 204, 34 × 2 = 68, and 204 + 68 = 272. Is he right? Explain his mistake and fix it.

Israel multiplicó 34 × 26 así: 34 × 6 = 204, 34 × 2 = 68 y 204 + 68 = 272. ¿Tiene razón? Explica su error y corrígelo.

Sentence starters: In 26, the 2 means ___. So the second row should be 34 × ___ = ___. Then 204 + ___ = ___.

Challenge: also check the product with an area model: 30 × 20, 30 × 6, 4 × 20 and 4 × 6.

Check your own work

Read your answers again. Press each sentence that is true about your work.

Part 1. Round 386 to 400 and 42 to 40. Then 400 × 40 = 16,000. Selena’s answer, 16,212, is close to 16,000, so it is reasonable. If she had gotten 1,621 or 162,120, the estimate would show that something was wrong.

Part 2. Israel is not right. In 26, the 2 is in the tens place, so it means 20, not 2. The second row should be 34 × 20 = 680. Then 204 + 680 = 884. He forgot the placeholder zero.

Challenge. The exact product is 386 × 42 = 16,212, so Selena was right. The area model for 34 × 26 is 600 + 180 + 80 + 24 = 884.

Aligned to the Illinois fifth grade mathematics standards 5.NBT.5 (fluently multiply multi-digit whole numbers using the standard algorithm) and 5.NBT.2 (explain patterns in the number of zeros of the product when multiplying a number by powers of 10). The games estimate before computing, connect the area model and partial products with the standard algorithm, and ask whether an answer is reasonable. At the Easy level the work uses 2-digit by 2-digit numbers with a visual model on screen (number lines for rounding, an area model drawn as a rectangle split in parts, the algorithm with each row labeled, an estimate next to each word problem, the pattern of zeros step by step); at the Challenge level the numbers grow to 3- and 4-digit factors, the supports are removed, word problems take two steps, the child finds the step where a classmate made a mistake, and in “Who is right?” must also choose the reason. The optional “Ayuda en español” button translates only the instruction or the question; the game itself stays in English. Each game has its own direct link: #estimar, #area, #algoritmo, #problemas, #patrones and #razon, and the final task (build and explain an answer without options) is #tarea.

Alineado con los estándares de matemáticas de Illinois para quinto grado: 5.NBT.5 (multiplicar con fluidez números enteros de varias cifras usando el algoritmo estándar) y 5.NBT.2 (explicar los patrones en la cantidad de ceros del producto al multiplicar un número por potencias de 10). En el nivel Fácil se trabaja con números de 2 cifras por 2 cifras y un modelo visual en pantalla; en el Reto los factores tienen 3 y 4 cifras, se quitan los apoyos, los problemas tienen dos pasos, hay que encontrar el error de un compañero y, en «¿Quién tiene razón?», elegir también la razón. Desde quinto grado los juegos son en inglés; el botón «Ayuda en español» traduce solo la consigna o la pregunta.
