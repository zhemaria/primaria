Word meanings and figurative language

🔍📖✨🧩

Words are full of surprises!

Good readers are word detectives. With Lumi you will use cause-and-effect and comparison clues to find what a word means, read dictionary, glossary and thesaurus entries, explain similes and metaphors, figure out idioms, adages and proverbs, tell homographs apart and choose the signal word that connects two ideas. Then you will prove your answers with the text. With Israel, Daniel, Elías, Sophia, Athena, Salma and Selena.

Los buenos lectores son detectives de palabras. Con Lumi vas a usar pistas de causa y efecto y de comparación para descubrir qué significa una palabra, leer entradas de diccionario, glosario y diccionario de sinónimos, explicar símiles y metáforas, entender modismos, dichos y refranes, distinguir homógrafos y escoger la palabra de enlace que une dos ideas. Después vas a demostrar tus respuestas con el texto.

Aligned to the Illinois fifth grade English Language Arts standards L.5.4a (use context, such as cause/effect relationships and comparisons in text, as a clue to the meaning of a word or phrase), L.5.4c (consult reference materials, both print and digital, such as dictionaries, glossaries and thesauruses, to find the pronunciation and determine or clarify the precise meaning of key words and phrases), L.5.5a (interpret figurative language, including similes and metaphors, in context), L.5.5b (recognize and explain the meaning of common idioms, adages and proverbs), L.5.5c (use the relationship between particular words, such as synonyms, antonyms and homographs, to better understand each of the words) and L.5.6 (acquire and use accurately grade-appropriate general academic and domain-specific words and phrases, including those that signal contrast, addition and other logical relationships, such as however, although, nevertheless, similarly, moreover and in addition). At the Easy level passages are shorter, clues are marked after a mistake and there are fewer choices; at the Challenge level passages are longer and the child must prove each answer: quote the exact words of the passage that give the clue, name the kind of figurative language and the two things compared, choose the situation in which a saying fits and explain the logical relationship a signal word shows. Every wrong answer comes from a typical mistake, and its hint explains it. The optional “Ayuda en español” button translates only the instruction or the question (never the passage or the answer choices), a WIDA-friendly support; the game itself stays in English. Each game has its own direct link: #pistas, #diccionario, #figurado, #dichos, #relaciones and #conectores.

Word meanings and figurative language

Name: ____________________________ Date: ____________

1. Read the passage. Underline the clue. What does meager mean? What kind of clue is it (cause/effect, comparison or contrast)?

It hardly rained from June to August, and the soil cracked like an old sidewalk. Because of the dry weather, the corn harvest on Grandpa Ruiz's farm was meager: he filled only two trucks instead of the usual ten.

2. Read the passage. Underline the clue. What does abundant mean? What kind of clue is it?

In the Desert House at the Garfield Park Conservatory, water is scarce and only a few spiky plants grow. In the Fern Room next door, by contrast, water is abundant. Mist sprays from the ceiling, and little streams run between the plants.

3. Write S (simile) or M (metaphor). Name the two things compared and explain what it means.

a) For Salma, the math test was a mountain, but she climbed it one problem at a time. ____
b) The Chicago skyline at night glittered like a jewelry box. ____

4. What does each saying mean? Describe a time when you could use one of them.

a) Don't count your chickens before they hatch. b) Break the ice.

5. The bold words are homographs. Write how each one is said (it rhymes with…) and what it means.

a) A tear rolled down Salma's cheek. b) Be careful not to tear the old map.

6. Complete with a signal word: however, moreover, similarly, as a result, for instance. Then name the relationship.

a) Israel practiced his trumpet every evening for a month. ____________, he earned first chair in the band.
b) The forecast called for heavy rain all day. ____________, the Cubs game started right on time.
