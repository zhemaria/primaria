Parentheses first, then crack the code!

Math has its own grammar. Parentheses ( ) and brackets [ ] tell you what to do first, and without them you multiply and divide before you add and subtract. With Lumi you will find the value of expressions, write the expression that tells a story, compare expressions without calculating, decide where the parentheses go, build two number patterns and graph them, and decide who is right. With Israel, Daniel, Elías, Sophia, Athena, Salma and Selena.

Las matemáticas tienen su propia gramática. Los paréntesis ( ) y los corchetes [ ] dicen qué se hace primero, y sin ellos se multiplica y se divide antes de sumar y restar. Con Lumi vas a hallar el valor de expresiones, escribir la expresión que cuenta una historia, comparar expresiones sin calcular, decidir dónde van los paréntesis, construir dos patrones de números y graficarlos, y decidir quién tiene razón.

Final task: explain it yourself

Part 1 · Explain

Athena says: “3 × (4 + 5) and 3 × 4 + 5 are not equal.” Explain why she is right.

Athena dice: «3 × (4 + 5) y 3 × 4 + 5 no son iguales». Explica por qué tiene razón.

Sentence starters: In 3 × (4 + 5), I first ___, so it is 3 × ___ = ___. In 3 × 4 + 5, I first ___, so it is ___ + 5 = ___. The parentheses ___.

Useful words: parentheses, brackets, order of operations, expression, evaluate, first.

Challenge: also evaluate 2 × [10 − (3 + 4)] and explain which part you do first.

Reto: calcula también 2 × [10 − (3 + 4)] y explica qué parte resuelves primero.

Part 2 · Apply the idea to a new problem

Daniel says: “3 × (452 + 87) is 3 times as large as 452 + 87, so I don’t need to calculate it.” Is he right? Explain, and write your own example.

Daniel dice: «3 × (452 + 87) es 3 veces mayor que 452 + 87, así que no necesito calcularlo». ¿Tiene razón? Explica y escribe tu propio ejemplo.

Sentence starters: Daniel is ___ because 3 × (452 + 87) means ___. Another example is ___.

Challenge: also write and evaluate an expression with parentheses, brackets and braces: add 2 and 3, multiply the sum by 4, subtract the result from 50, then divide by 3.

Reto: escribe y calcula también una expresión con paréntesis, corchetes y llaves: suma 2 y 3, multiplica la suma por 4, resta el resultado de 50 y luego divide entre 3.

Check your own work

Read your answers again. Press each sentence that is true about your work.

Vuelve a leer tus respuestas. Pulsa cada oración que sea verdadera sobre tu trabajo.

Part 1. Athena is right. In 3 × (4 + 5), the parentheses tell you to add first: 4 + 5 = 9, and 3 × 9 = 27. In 3 × 4 + 5, there are no parentheses, so you multiply first: 3 × 4 = 12, and 12 + 5 = 17. Since 27 is not 17, the expressions are not equal. Parentheses change the order of the operations.

Part 2. Daniel is right. 3 × (452 + 87) means 3 groups of (452 + 87), so it is 3 times as large as 452 + 87, without calculating the sum. Another example: 5 × (18 + 6) is 5 times as large as 18 + 6.

Challenge. In 2 × [10 − (3 + 4)], work from the inside out: first the parentheses, 3 + 4 = 7; then the brackets, 10 − 7 = 3; then 2 × 3 = 6. With braces: {50 − [4 × (2 + 3)]} ÷ 3 = {50 − 20} ÷ 3 = 30 ÷ 3 = 10.

Your words can be different. What matters is that your answer shows the math and explains how it proves your answer.

Your answers are not saved. Show them to your teacher or your family, or use the printed page.

Tus respuestas no se guardan. Muéstraselas a tu maestra o a tu familia, o usa la hoja impresa.

Aligned to the Illinois fifth grade mathematics standards 5.OA.1 (use parentheses, brackets, or braces in numerical expressions, and evaluate expressions with these symbols), 5.OA.2 (write simple expressions that record calculations with numbers, and interpret numerical expressions without evaluating them; for example, express the calculation “add 8 and 7, then multiply by 2” as 2 × (8 + 7), and recognize that 3 × (18932 + 921) is three times as large as 18932 + 921, without having to calculate the indicated sum or product) and 5.OA.3 (generate two numerical patterns using two given rules; identify apparent relationships between corresponding terms; form ordered pairs consisting of corresponding terms from the two patterns, and graph the ordered pairs on a coordinate plane). Every expression comes with a situation, and the games show that without parentheses the usual order (× and ÷ before + and −) gives a different result. At the Easy level the part inside the parentheses is highlighted with the first step, bar models show the groups, and the patterns appear in a table; at the Challenge level there are brackets inside brackets, bigger numbers, patterns as lists, graphs of ordered pairs, and in “Who is right?” the child must also choose the reason. The optional “Ayuda en español” button translates only the instruction or the question; the game itself stays in English. Each game has its own direct link: #evaluar, #escribir, #interpretar, #parentesis, #patrones and #razon, and the final task (build and explain an answer without options) is #tarea.

Alineado con los estándares de matemáticas de Illinois para quinto grado: 5.OA.1 (usar paréntesis, corchetes o llaves en expresiones numéricas y evaluar expresiones con estos símbolos), 5.OA.2 (escribir expresiones sencillas que registren cálculos con números e interpretar expresiones numéricas sin evaluarlas; por ejemplo, escribir «suma 8 y 7, luego multiplica por 2» como 2 × (8 + 7) y reconocer que 3 × (18932 + 921) es tres veces mayor que 18932 + 921 sin calcular la suma ni el producto) y 5.OA.3 (generar dos patrones numéricos a partir de dos reglas dadas, identificar relaciones entre los términos correspondientes, formar pares ordenados con esos términos y graficarlos en un plano de coordenadas). Cada expresión viene con una situación. En el nivel Fácil se resalta lo que está dentro de los paréntesis y los patrones aparecen en una tabla; en el Reto hay corchetes, números más grandes y gráficas. Desde quinto grado los juegos son en inglés; el botón «Ayuda en español» traduce solo la consigna o la pregunta.
