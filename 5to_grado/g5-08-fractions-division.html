Fractions as division

Share it fair and square!

A fraction is also a division: 3/4 means 3 ÷ 4. When 4 friends share 3 pizzas equally, each friend gets 3/4 of a pizza. With Lumi you will share pizzas and brownies, write divisions as fractions, turn improper fractions into mixed numbers, find between which two whole numbers an answer lies, solve word problems and decide who is right. With Israel, Daniel, Elías, Sophia, Athena, Salma and Selena.

Una fracción también es una división: 3/4 quiere decir 3 ÷ 4. Si 4 amigos se reparten 3 pizzas en partes iguales, cada uno recibe 3/4 de pizza. Con Lumi vas a repartir pizzas y brownies, escribir divisiones como fracciones, convertir fracciones impropias en números mixtos, descubrir entre qué dos números enteros está una respuesta, resolver problemas y decidir quién tiene razón.

Part 1 · Explain

Three friends share 2 pizzas equally. Israel says each friend gets 2/3 of a pizza. Explain why he is right. You can draw a model in your notebook.

Tres amigos se reparten 2 pizzas en partes iguales. Israel dice que a cada uno le toca 2/3 de pizza. Explica por qué tiene razón. Puedes dibujar un modelo en tu cuaderno.

Sentence starters: I cut each pizza into ___ equal pieces. There are ___ pieces in all. Each friend gets ___ pieces, which is ___ of a pizza. So 2 ÷ 3 = ___.

Useful words: share equally, divide, numerator, denominator, quotient.

Challenge: also explain why 2 ÷ 3 and 2/3 are the same number.

Reto: explica también por qué 2 ÷ 3 y 2/3 son el mismo número.

Part 2 · Apply the idea to a new problem

Sophia has 17 feet of ribbon to cut into 5 equal pieces. She says each piece is 3 feet long, with 2 feet left over. Is that the best answer? Explain, and give the exact length of each piece.

Sophia tiene 17 pies de cinta para cortar en 5 trozos iguales. Dice que cada trozo mide 3 pies y que sobran 2 pies. ¿Es la mejor respuesta? Explica y da la longitud exacta de cada trozo.

Sentence starters: 17 ÷ 5 = 3 R ___. The ___ feet left over can also be shared among the 5 pieces, so each piece gets ___ more. Each piece is ___ feet long.

Challenge: also tell between which two whole numbers the answer is, and how you know.

Reto: di también entre qué dos números enteros está la respuesta y cómo lo sabes.

Check your own work

Read your answers again. Press each sentence that is true about your work.

Vuelve a leer tus respuestas. Pulsa cada oración que sea verdadera sobre tu trabajo.

Part 1. Cut each pizza into 3 equal pieces. There are 6 pieces in all, and each of the 3 friends gets 2 pieces. Each piece is 1/3 of a pizza, so each friend gets 2/3 of a pizza. That shows that 2 ÷ 3 = 2/3.

Part 2. Sophia’s answer leaves ribbon unused. The 2 feet left over can also be shared: 2 ÷ 5 = 2/5 foot more for each piece. So each piece is exactly 17/5 = 3 2/5 feet long.

Challenge. A fraction a/b means a ÷ b: the numerator is divided by the denominator. 3 2/5 is between 3 and 4, because 5 × 3 = 15 and 5 × 4 = 20, and 17 is between 15 and 20.

Your words can be different. What matters is that your answer shows the math and explains how it proves your answer.

Aligned to the Illinois fifth grade mathematics standard 5.NF.3 (interpret a fraction as division of the numerator by the denominator, a/b = a ÷ b; solve word problems involving division of whole numbers leading to answers in the form of fractions or mixed numbers, e.g., by using visual fraction models or equations to represent the problem; for example, if 9 people want to share a 50-pound sack of rice equally by weight, how many pounds of rice should each person get? Between what two whole numbers does your answer lie?). At the Easy level a model is on screen: pizzas, sandwiches and brownie pans cut and dealt out, fraction bars grouped into wholes, a double number line with the multiples of the divisor, and an estimate before each word problem; at the Challenge level the work is with the numbers alone, with bigger numbers, two-step word problems, mixed numbers in both directions, and in “Who is right?” the child must also choose the reason. Fractions appear stacked, and the voice reads them the way they are said in class (“three fourths”, “three and two fifths”). The optional “Ayuda en español” button translates only the instruction or the question; the game itself stays in English. Each game has its own direct link: #compartir, #escribir, #mixto, #entre, #problemas and #razon, and the final task (build and explain an answer without options) is #tarea.

Alineado con el estándar de matemáticas de Illinois para quinto grado 5.NF.3 (interpretar una fracción como la división del numerador entre el denominador, a/b = a ÷ b; resolver problemas verbales de división de números enteros cuyas respuestas son fracciones o números mixtos, por ejemplo con modelos visuales de fracciones o ecuaciones; por ejemplo, si 9 personas se reparten por igual un saco de arroz de 50 libras, ¿cuántas libras recibe cada una? ¿Entre qué dos números enteros está la respuesta?). En el nivel Fácil hay un modelo en pantalla; en el Reto se trabaja solo con los números, con problemas de dos pasos, y en «¿Quién tiene razón?» también hay que elegir la razón. Desde quinto grado los juegos son en inglés; el botón «Ayuda en español» traduce solo la consigna o la pregunta.
