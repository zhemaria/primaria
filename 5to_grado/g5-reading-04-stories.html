Israel and his friends Daniel, Elías, Sophia, Athena, Salma and Selena are eleven now, and their Chicago adventures fill stories, poems and short plays: a soccer tryout, a snow day newspaper, a swim in the lake, a museum trip, a talent show and visits with their grandparents. With Lumi you will read along, quote the text accurately, find the theme, compare characters, see how chapters, scenes and stanzas fit together and notice how the narrator changes the story.

Aligned to the Illinois fifth grade English Language Arts standards RL.5.1 (quote accurately from a text when explaining what the text says explicitly and when drawing inferences from the text), RL.5.2 (determine a theme of a story, drama or poem from details in the text, including how characters respond to challenges or how the speaker in a poem reflects upon a topic; summarize the text), RL.5.3 (compare and contrast two or more characters, settings or events in a story or drama, drawing on specific details in the text), RL.5.4 (determine the meaning of words and phrases as they are used in a text, including figurative language such as metaphors and similes), RL.5.5 (explain how a series of chapters, scenes or stanzas fits together to provide the overall structure of a particular story, drama or poem), RL.5.6 (describe how a narrator's or speaker's point of view influences how events are described) and RF.5.4 (read with sufficient accuracy and fluency to support comprehension). The eleven passages are original: seven stories, two poems and two short plays, plus five pairs of first-person accounts of the same event. Lumi can read every text aloud, highlighting each sentence. At the Easy level the texts are shorter, Lumi reads automatically in “Read along”, there are three choices and, after a mistake, the sentence with the evidence is marked; at the Challenge level the texts are longer, there are four choices and the child must prove the answer by picking the exact sentence from the text (or, in “Quote it”, the accurate quotation among misquotes and true but unhelpful quotes). Every wrong answer comes from a typical mistake (a paraphrase that changes the meaning, a topic or detail instead of a theme, a summary with opinions, a similarity offered as a difference, a detail the narrator could not know) and has its own hint. The optional “Ayuda en español” button translates only the instruction or the question; the passages, the choices and the hints stay in English, so the help can be used at either level (consistent with WIDA guidance for multilingual readers). Each game has its own direct link: #escucha, #citar, #tema, #comparar, #estructura and #narrador.

Alineado con los estándares de Artes del Lenguaje en inglés de Illinois para quinto grado: RL.5.1 (citar el texto con exactitud al explicar lo que dice de forma explícita y al hacer inferencias), RL.5.2 (determinar el tema de un cuento, una obra de teatro o un poema a partir de sus detalles, incluso cómo los personajes enfrentan los retos o cómo el hablante de un poema reflexiona sobre un asunto; resumir el texto), RL.5.3 (comparar y contrastar dos o más personajes, escenarios o sucesos con detalles concretos del texto), RL.5.4 (determinar el significado de palabras y frases, incluido el lenguaje figurado como metáforas y símiles), RL.5.5 (explicar cómo encajan los capítulos, las escenas o las estrofas en la estructura de un cuento, una obra o un poema), RL.5.6 (describir cómo el punto de vista del narrador o del hablante influye en la manera de contar los sucesos) y RF.5.4 (leer con exactitud y fluidez suficientes para comprender). Desde quinto grado los juegos son en inglés; el botón «Ayuda en español» traduce solo la consigna o la pregunta.
