Find your way on the grid!

Two number lines that cross at 0 make a map for numbers: the coordinate plane. A point like (3, 5) means 3 across and 5 up, and the order matters! With Lumi you will read and plot points, find places on a neighborhood map, discover which shapes belong to which families (every square is a rectangle!) and decide who is right. With Israel, Daniel, Elías, Sophia, Athena, Salma and Selena.

Dos rectas numéricas que se cruzan en 0 forman un mapa para los números: el plano de coordenadas. Un punto como (3, 5) quiere decir 3 hacia la derecha y 5 hacia arriba, ¡y el orden importa! Con Lumi vas a leer y marcar puntos, encontrar lugares en el mapa de un barrio, descubrir a qué familias pertenece cada figura (¡todo cuadrado es un rectángulo!) y decidir quién tiene razón.

Final task: explain it yourself

There are no options to choose from this time. Build your own answer and explain it, the way a mathematician would. Use what you know about the attributes of shapes: their sides, their angles and their parallel sides.

Esta vez no hay opciones para elegir. Construye tu propia respuesta y explícala, como lo haría un matemático. Usa lo que sabes sobre los atributos de las figuras: sus lados, sus ángulos y sus lados paralelos.

Part 1 · Explain

Israel says: “Every square is a rectangle.” Explain why he is right.

Israel dice: «Todo cuadrado es un rectángulo». Explica por qué tiene razón.

Sentence starters: A rectangle is a quadrilateral with ___. A square has ___. So a square ___.

Useful words: quadrilateral, right angle, opposite sides, parallel, equal, attribute.

Challenge: write your explanation without sentence starters, and use the word “attribute”.

Part 2 · Apply the idea to a new shape

Salma says: “Every rhombus is a square.” Is she right? Explain, and describe or draw a shape that proves your answer.

Salma dice: «Todo rombo es un cuadrado». ¿Tiene razón? Explica y describe o dibuja una figura que demuestre tu respuesta.

Sentence starters: Salma is ___ because a rhombus only needs ___. For example, ___.

Challenge: also tell which shapes can be both a rhombus and a rectangle.

Check your own work

Read your answers again. Press each sentence that is true about your work.

Vuelve a leer tus respuestas. Pulsa cada oración que sea verdadera sobre tu trabajo.

Part 1. A rectangle is a quadrilateral with four right angles. A square also has four right angles, so it has every attribute a rectangle needs. Its four equal sides are an extra attribute. That is why every square is a rectangle: a special rectangle.

Part 2. Salma is not right. A rhombus only needs four equal sides. Its angles do not have to be right angles, like the rhombus in the picture, which leans. That rhombus is not a square. Some rhombuses are squares, but not every rhombus is.

Challenge. Only a square can be both a rhombus and a rectangle, because it has four equal sides and four right angles.

Your words can be different. What matters is that your answer uses attributes and explains how they prove it.

Aligned to the Illinois fifth grade mathematics standards 5.G.1 (use a pair of perpendicular number lines, called axes, to define a coordinate system, with the intersection of the lines (the origin) arranged to coincide with the 0 on each line and a given point in the plane located by using an ordered pair of numbers, called its coordinates; understand that the first number indicates how far to travel from the origin in the direction of one axis, and the second number indicates how far to travel in the direction of the second axis, with the convention that the names of the two axes and the coordinates correspond: x-axis and x-coordinate, y-axis and y-coordinate), 5.G.2 (represent real world and mathematical problems by graphing points in the first quadrant of the coordinate plane, and interpret coordinate values of points in the context of the situation), 5.G.3 (understand that attributes belonging to a category of two-dimensional figures also belong to all subcategories of that category; for example, all rectangles have four right angles and squares are rectangles, so all squares have four right angles) and 5.G.4 (classify two-dimensional figures in a hierarchy based on properties). At the Easy level the grid shows guide lines and steps, the maps ask one-step questions and every shape is drawn with tick marks, right-angle marks and parallel arrows; at the Challenge level points sit on the axes or must be found from another point, map questions take two steps, the child picks all the names or shapes that apply, and in “Who is right?” the child must also choose the reason. These games use the inclusive definition of a trapezoid: a quadrilateral with at least one pair of parallel sides, so every parallelogram is also a trapezoid. On the “Plot the point” grid every intersection is a button, so it can be played with the keyboard. The optional “Ayuda en español” button translates only the instruction or the question; the game itself stays in English. Each game has its own direct link: #leer, #ubicar, #mapa, #atributos, #jerarquia and #razon, and the final task (build and explain an answer without options) is #tarea.

Alineado con los estándares de matemáticas de Illinois para quinto grado: 5.G.1 (usar un par de rectas numéricas perpendiculares, llamadas ejes, para definir un sistema de coordenadas cuyo origen coincide con el 0 de cada recta; entender que el primer número indica cuánto avanzar desde el origen en la dirección del eje x y el segundo, cuánto avanzar en la dirección del eje y), 5.G.2 (representar problemas de la vida real y matemáticos marcando puntos en el primer cuadrante e interpretar las coordenadas según la situación), 5.G.3 (entender que los atributos de una categoría de figuras planas también pertenecen a todas sus subcategorías: todo rectángulo tiene cuatro ángulos rectos y los cuadrados son rectángulos, así que todo cuadrado tiene cuatro ángulos rectos) y 5.G.4 (clasificar figuras planas en una jerarquía según sus propiedades). Se usa la definición inclusiva de trapecio: un cuadrilátero con al menos un par de lados paralelos. Desde quinto grado los juegos son en inglés; el botón «Ayuda en español» traduce solo la consigna o la pregunta.
