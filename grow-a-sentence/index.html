For the grown-up helping out
  • The big idea: a sentence grows in layers. The child sees that A dog is not a sentence yet (it needs an action), and that every new question adds information, not just extra words.
  • Every choice narrows the next one. The character decides which actions appear — a cat can sleep, purr, climb, hide, jump or eat, but it will never be offered dances. The action then decides the places, times and reasons. A sentence can come out silly, but never nonsense.
  • The colors do a job. Each part keeps its color in the sentence, on the sticky note and in the step strip, so the child links blue = where and purple = when.
  • Ask before they tap: "Where do you think a girl eats?" The button only confirms what they already said out loud.
  • The grammar moment is step 4: moving the time to the front brings in a comma, and A dog becomes a dog. Point at it with your finger while it changes.
  • To edit the word lists: open the file and find WHO, PLACES, TIMES and ACTIONS at the top of the script. ACTIONS is grouped by kind of character, and each action names the places, times and reasons that belong to it.
  • Accessibility: works with the keyboard (Tab and Enter), reads aloud, honors the system setting for reduced motion, and prints cleanly with Ctrl+P showing only the picture and the sentence.
