Turns and angles!

A full turn is 360°, a quarter turn is a right angle, and the corner of a sheet of paper helps you compare. With Lumi you will sort angles, measure turns on a clock, read a protractor, estimate, add and subtract the parts of an angle, and find angles in scissors, slides and books. With Israel, Daniel, Elías, Sophia, Athena, Salma and Selena.

Aligned to the Illinois fourth grade mathematics standards 4.MD.5 (recognize angles as shapes formed by two rays with a common endpoint and understand angle measure as a fraction of a full turn: a 1° angle is 1/360 of a turn), 4.MD.6 (measure angles in whole-number degrees using a protractor), 4.MD.7 (recognize angle measure as additive and solve addition and subtraction problems to find unknown angles, writing an equation with a letter for the unknown) and 4.G.1 (identify right, acute and obtuse angles). At the Easy level there are visual supports: the corner of a sheet of paper as a right angle, the little square that marks 90°, the shaded circle for each turn, the protractor's 0 always on the right, dotted 90° and 180° benchmark lines and labeled parts. At the Challenge level the angles are close to 90° with no marker, there are clocks with the minute hand and the hour hand, the protractor is read from either side in steps of 5, the estimates are closer, some angles are split into three parts, and the steps stay on screen. The question “Can that be?” asks the child to analyze a classmate's reasoning. Each game has its own direct link: #tipo, #giro, #transportador, #estimar, #sumar and #dibujar.
