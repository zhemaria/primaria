Aligned to the Illinois fourth grade reading standards RL.4.1 (refer to details and examples in a text when explaining what the text says explicitly and when drawing inferences), RL.4.2 (determine a theme from details in the text and summarize the text), RL.4.3 (describe in depth a character, setting or event, drawing on specific details such as a character's thoughts, words and actions), RL.4.5 (explain major differences between poems, drama and prose, and refer to their structural elements: verse, rhythm and meter; casts of characters, settings, dialogue and stage directions), RL.4.6 (compare and contrast the point of view from which different stories are narrated, including the difference between first- and third-person narrations) and RF.4.4 (read with purpose, understanding and fluency). Lumi can read the text aloud, highlighting each sentence. At the Easy level the text is read automatically in the first game, the sentence with the answer is marked after a mistake and there are fewer choices; at the Challenge level the texts are longer, the child reads first and must prove the answer by choosing the sentence or feature that supports it, and in Who tells the story? compares two versions of the same event told in the first and third person. Every wrong answer comes from a typical mistake (confusing the theme with the topic or a detail, a summary with opinions or too many details, a verse with a stanza), and its hint explains it. Separately from the level, the Spanish help button (off at the start) shows a translation under each question; the text and the answers stay in English. This keeps reading difficulty and home-language support apart, so the help can also be used at the Challenge level (consistent with WIDA guidance for multilingual readers). Each game has its own direct link: #escucha, #inferir, #tema, #personajes, #formas and #narrador.
