Twelve game modules

Lumi is now in fourth grade and challenges you a little more. Here you will play with numbers up to a million, multiply and divide big numbers, fractions, decimals, measurement, angles and shapes. You can listen to every instruction and answer by touching. You can play all by yourself!

For the teacher and the family

The twelve modules cover the Illinois fourth grade mathematics standards (Illinois Learning Standards: 4.OA, 4.NBT, 4.NF, 4.MD and 4.G). They can be used in any order, although the list follows the usual sequence of the year: first place value and operations with large numbers, then fractions and decimals, and finally measurement, angles and shapes.

What changes in fourth grade

In third grade the games already asked for strategy; in fourth grade the child also has to justify. The same ideas continue one step further, and the challenge should fit what each child can do, not only their grade.

  • Analyze someone else's reasoning: in “Who is right?” a classmate explains an answer and the child decides whether it is right and why.
  • Choose the strategy: to compare fractions the child can find a common denominator or use 1/2 as a benchmark; the game accepts both paths and Lumi explains the one that fits best.
  • Steps on screen: at the Challenge level, the steps of the procedure stay on screen so the child can follow them independently.
  • Mark and check: some games ask the child to find every answer that works, not just one.
  • Explain decisions: after a correct answer Lumi retells the path, and at the end of each game it asks “Which strategy did you use? What would you change?” without grading the answer.
  • For collaboration: play in pairs; one reads and proposes, the other checks and explains whether they agree.

How they are designed

  • Every instruction is spoken, and the speaker button repeats it as often as needed; word problems and reasoning are read aloud too.
  • Meaning first, procedure second: bars of the same length show why two fractions are equivalent before working with the numbers alone.
  • Mistakes are not penalized: a gentle tone, a hint and another try. When the mistake is a typical one, such as adding the same number to the top and the bottom, the hint explains it.
  • Each game has two levels, Easy and Challenge, so the same page can stay with the child all year.
  • Each game has its own direct link with an anchor (#), to send the child straight to the game of the day.
  • Everything works with a keyboard and a screen reader, respects reduced motion and stores no child data. Each module includes a printable page.
