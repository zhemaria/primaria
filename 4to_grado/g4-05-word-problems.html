Aligned to the Illinois fourth grade mathematics standards 4.OA.1 (interpret a multiplication equation as a comparison, e.g., interpret 35 = 5 × 7 as a statement that 35 is 5 times as many as 7 and 7 times as many as 5, and represent verbal statements of multiplicative comparisons as equations), 4.OA.2 (multiply or divide to solve word problems involving multiplicative comparison, using drawings and equations with a symbol for the unknown number, distinguishing multiplicative comparison from additive comparison) and 4.OA.3 (solve multistep word problems posed with whole numbers using the four operations, including problems in which remainders must be interpreted, represent these problems using equations with a letter standing for the unknown quantity, and assess the reasonableness of answers using mental computation and estimation strategies including rounding). At the Easy level there are dot groups, bar models, the numbers in the problem highlighted, skip counting for division and the rounding on screen; at the Challenge level there are equations with a letter, two-step equations with parentheses, three-step problems and two-step remainder problems with the steps on screen, and the child chooses the best estimate before deciding. Wrong options come from typical mistakes (adding instead of multiplying when reading “times as many”, stopping after the first step, using the wrong operation, leaving out a quantity, writing an extra zero) and each one has its own hint. The game “Is it reasonable?” asks the child to analyze a classmate's answer. Each game has its own direct link: #veces, #comparar, #ecuacion, #pasos, #residuo and #razonable.
