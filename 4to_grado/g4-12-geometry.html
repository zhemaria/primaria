Lines, angles and shapes are everywhere: on a ladder, on train tracks, on city streets and on the wings of a butterfly. Aligned to the Illinois fourth grade mathematics standards 4.G.1 (draw and identify points, lines, line segments, rays, right, acute and obtuse angles, and perpendicular and parallel lines), 4.G.2 (classify two-dimensional figures based on the presence or absence of parallel or perpendicular lines and of angles of a specified size, and recognize right triangles) and 4.G.3 (recognize a line of symmetry as a line across the figure such that it can be folded into matching parts, identify line-symmetric figures and draw lines of symmetry). The inclusive definition is used: a trapezoid has at least one pair of parallel sides, so every parallelogram is also a trapezoid; and a square is also a rectangle, a rhombus and a parallelogram. At the Easy level the shapes carry marks (a small square for a right angle, ticks for equal sides and arrows for parallel sides) and, after a correct answer, the fold of the shape is shown; at the Challenge level figures are named with letters (line segment AB, ray CD), lines are found in everyday pictures, every name of a shape is marked, lines of symmetry are counted and the line of symmetry can be horizontal or diagonal. The games “Classify the shape” and “Who am I?” ask the child to justify with properties. Each game has its own direct link: #rectas, #paralelas, #clasificar, #propiedades, #simetria and #completar.

Lines, shapes and symmetry

Name: ____________________________ Date: ____________

1. Write whether each figure is a line, a line segment or a ray, with its letters (for example, line segment CD).

2. Draw a ray that starts at point P, and a pair of parallel lines.

3. Trace the parallel sides with a color. Mark the right angles with a small square. Fill in.

4. Write the best name for each shape.

5. Draw all the lines of symmetry of each shape and write how many it has.

6. Is a square also a rectangle? Explain why.
