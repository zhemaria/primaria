The world of decimals!

Three tenths are thirty hundredths, and 75 cents is 0.75 of a dollar. With Lumi you will change tenths into hundredths, write fractions as decimals, find decimals on the number line, compare them, count money and decide who is right. With Israel, Daniel, Elías, Sophia, Athena, Salma and Selena.

Aligned to the Illinois fourth grade mathematics standards 4.NF.5 (express a fraction with denominator 10 as an equivalent fraction with denominator 100, and use this to add two fractions with denominators 10 and 100), 4.NF.6 (use decimal notation for fractions with denominators 10 or 100, and locate decimals on a number line) and 4.NF.7 (compare two decimals to hundredths by reasoning about their size, recognizing that comparisons are valid only when they refer to the same whole, and record the result with >, = or <). At the Easy level there are hundredths grids, strips of 10 tenths, number lines with jumps and pictures of coins; at the Challenge level the work is with the numbers, with the steps on screen, with mixed numbers such as 3 25/100 = 3.25 and with prices at the school store. The voice reads decimals as fractions (“seventy-five hundredths”). The game “Who is right?” asks the child to analyze a classmate's reasoning. Each game has its own direct link: #decimos, #escribir, #recta, #comparar, #dinero and #razon.

2. Write each fraction as a decimal, and each decimal as a fraction.
