Part 1 · Explain

The sixth grade holds a car wash and charges $8 for each car. Name the independent variable and the dependent variable, and explain how you decided. Make a table for 0, 1, 2, 3 and 4 cars, and write an equation for the money m earned for c cars.

Sexto grado organiza un lavado de carros y cobra $8 por cada carro. Di cuál es la variable independiente y cuál la dependiente, y explica cómo lo decidiste. Haz una tabla para 0, 1, 2, 3 y 4 carros y escribe una ecuación para el dinero m que se gana con c carros.

Sentence starters: The money ___ on the number of cars, so ___ is the dependent variable and ___ is the independent variable. Each car adds $___, so m = ___. For 4 cars, m = ___.

Useful words: independent variable, dependent variable, depends on, table, equation, graph, rule.

Challenge: the class pays $20 for soap before they start. Write a new equation for the profit p, and find how many cars they must wash to have a profit of $100.

Reto: la clase paga $20 por el jabón antes de empezar. Escribe una nueva ecuación para la ganancia p y halla cuántos carros deben lavar para ganar $100.

Part 2 · Apply the idea to a new problem

Daniel says: “The graph of m = 8c is a straight line that starts at (0, 0) and goes up 8 for every 1 to the right.” Is he right? Explain using your table. Then describe another situation from your own life with an independent and a dependent variable.

Daniel dice: «La gráfica de m = 8c es una recta que empieza en (0, 0) y sube 8 por cada 1 hacia la derecha». ¿Tiene razón? Explica con tu tabla. Después describe otra situación de tu propia vida con una variable independiente y una dependiente.

Sentence starters: The points of my table are (0, ___), (1, ___), ___. Each step to the right, m goes up ___. So Daniel ___. In my situation, ___ depends on ___.

Challenge: does the graph of the profit p = 8c − 20 also start at (0, 0)? Explain what its first point means.

Reto: ¿la gráfica de la ganancia p = 8c − 20 también empieza en (0, 0)? Explica qué significa su primer punto.

Part 1. The money depends on the number of cars, so the money m is the dependent variable and the number of cars c is the independent variable. I decided by asking “which one changes because of the other?”: washing more cars brings more money, not the other way around. Table: 0 cars → $0, 1 → $8, 2 → $16, 3 → $24, 4 → $32. Each car adds $8, so m = 8c.

Part 2. Daniel is right. The points (0, 0), (1, 8), (2, 16), (3, 24), (4, 32) line up: each step of 1 to the right goes up 8, and with 0 cars there is $0, so the line starts at (0, 0). My situation: the time I spend reading (independent) and the number of pages I read (dependent).

Challenge. The profit is p = 8c − 20. For a profit of $100: 8c − 20 = 100, so 8c = 120 and c = 15 cars. The graph of p = 8c − 20 does not start at (0, 0): its first point is (0, −20), which means that before washing any car the class has spent $20 and has lost money.

Aligned to the Illinois sixth grade mathematics standard 6.EE.9: use variables to represent two quantities in a real-world problem that change in relationship to one another; write an equation to express one quantity, thought of as the dependent variable, in terms of the other quantity, thought of as the independent variable; and analyze the relationship using graphs and tables, and relate these to the equation (for example, in a problem with constant speed, list and graph ordered pairs of distances and times, and write d = 65t). At the Easy level the rules are of the form y = kx or y = x + k, the tables show the change from one row to the next, and the graphs have few points; at the Challenge level the rules can be y = kx + b, the child finds the rule from a table, reads values from a graph, chooses the graph that tells a story, and in “Who is right?” must also choose the reason. Typical mistakes get their own hints: switching the dependent and independent variables, finding the rule from the first row only, and using the change between rows as the whole rule. The optional “Ayuda en español” button translates only the instruction or the question; the game itself stays in English. Alineado con el estándar de matemáticas de Illinois para sexto grado 6.EE.9: usar variables para representar dos cantidades que cambian una en relación con la otra en un problema real; escribir una ecuación que exprese la cantidad dependiente en función de la independiente; y analizar la relación con gráficas y tablas y relacionarlas con la ecuación (por ejemplo, con velocidad constante, anotar y graficar pares de distancias y tiempos y escribir d = 65t). En el nivel Fácil las reglas son de la forma y = kx o y = x + k, las tablas muestran el cambio de una fila a otra y las gráficas tienen pocos puntos; en el Reto las reglas pueden ser y = kx + b, se halla la regla a partir de una tabla, se leen valores en una gráfica y se elige la gráfica que cuenta una historia. Los errores típicos tienen su propia pista: cambiar la variable dependiente por la independiente, hallar la regla solo con la primera fila y usar el cambio entre filas como si fuera toda la regla. Desde quinto grado los juegos son en inglés; el botón «Ayuda en español» traduce solo la consigna o la pregunta.
