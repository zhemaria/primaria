Part 1 · Explain

Athena wraps a gift box that is 10 inches long, 6 inches wide and 4 inches tall. She says: “I need 240 square inches of paper, because 10 × 6 × 4 = 240.” Is she right? Draw or describe the net, find the surface area, and explain what 240 really measures.

Athena envuelve una caja de regalo de 10 pulgadas de largo, 6 de ancho y 4 de alto. Dice: «Necesito 240 pulgadas cuadradas de papel, porque 10 × 6 × 4 = 240». ¿Tiene razón? Dibuja o describe la plantilla, halla el área de superficie y explica qué mide realmente 240.

Sentence starters: The net has ___ rectangles: two of ___ × ___, two of ___ × ___ and two of ___ × ___. The surface area is ___ + ___ + ___ = ___ square inches. 10 × 6 × 4 = 240 is the ___, measured in ___.

Useful words: net, face, surface area, volume, square units, cubic units.

Challenge: the box is filled with cubes of 1/2 inch on each side. How many cubes fit? Show that you get the same volume with V = l × w × h.

Reto: la caja se llena con cubos de 1/2 pulgada de arista. ¿Cuántos cubos caben? Muestra que obtienes el mismo volumen con V = l × w × h.

Part 2 · Apply the idea to a new problem

A garden bed is shaped like a triangle with a base of 8 feet and a height of 5 feet. Daniel says its area is 40 square feet. Is he right? Explain using a rectangle. Then find the area of a parallelogram with base 8 feet and height 5 feet, and explain why it is different.

Un cantero tiene forma de triángulo con una base de 8 pies y una altura de 5 pies. Daniel dice que su área es de 40 pies cuadrados. ¿Tiene razón? Explica usando un rectángulo. Después halla el área de un paralelogramo de base 8 pies y altura 5 pies y explica por qué es diferente.

Sentence starters: The triangle is ___ of a rectangle that is ___ by ___. So its area is ___ × ___ ÷ 2 = ___. The parallelogram can be cut and moved to make a ___, so its area is ___.

Challenge: a trapezoid has parallel sides of 6 feet and 10 feet and a height of 4 feet. Find its area by cutting it into a rectangle and triangles, or by doubling it into a parallelogram.

Reto: un trapecio tiene lados paralelos de 6 y 10 pies y una altura de 4 pies. Halla su área dividiéndolo en un rectángulo y triángulos, o duplicándolo para formar un paralelogramo.

Part 1. Athena is not right. The net of the box has 6 rectangles: two of 10 × 6 (top and bottom), two of 10 × 4 (front and back) and two of 6 × 4 (the sides). The surface area is 2 × 60 + 2 × 40 + 2 × 24 = 120 + 80 + 48 = 248 square inches. 10 × 6 × 4 = 240 is the volume, the space inside the box, measured in cubic inches, not the paper on the outside.

Part 2. Daniel is not right. The triangle is half of a rectangle that is 8 by 5, so its area is 8 × 5 ÷ 2 = 20 square feet; 40 is the area of the whole rectangle. A parallelogram with base 8 and height 5 can be cut and the piece moved to make an 8 by 5 rectangle, so its area is 40 square feet: two of those triangles fit in it.

Challenge. Each inch holds 2 half-inch cubes, so the box holds 20 × 12 × 8 = 1,920 small cubes. Each small cube is 1/2 × 1/2 × 1/2 = 1/8 cubic inch, and 1,920 × 1/8 = 240 cubic inches, the same as 10 × 6 × 4. The trapezoid: two copies make a parallelogram with base 6 + 10 = 16 and height 4, area 64, so the trapezoid is 64 ÷ 2 = 32 square feet.

Aligned to the Illinois sixth grade mathematics standards 6.G.1 (find the area of right triangles, other triangles, special quadrilaterals and polygons by composing into rectangles or decomposing into triangles and other shapes), 6.G.2 (find the volume of a right rectangular prism with fractional edge lengths by packing it with unit cubes of the appropriate unit fraction edge lengths, and show that the volume is the same as multiplying the edge lengths; apply V = l w h and V = b h), 6.G.3 (draw polygons in the coordinate plane given coordinates for the vertices; use coordinates to find the length of a side joining points with the same first or the same second coordinate) and 6.G.4 (represent three-dimensional figures using nets made up of rectangles and triangles, and use the nets to find the surface area of these figures). At the Easy level every figure is drawn with its base and height, the triangle appears inside its rectangle, and the nets are labeled; at the Challenge level there are fractional and decimal measures, trapezoids and composite figures, polygons given only by coordinates, missing dimensions, and in “Who is right?” the child must also choose the reason. Typical mistakes get their own hints: forgetting to halve the area of a triangle, using the slanted side instead of the height, confusing surface area with volume, and counting only three faces of a box. Los errores típicos tienen su propia pista: olvidar dividir entre 2 en el triángulo, usar el lado inclinado en lugar de la altura, confundir el área de superficie con el volumen y contar solo tres caras de una caja.
