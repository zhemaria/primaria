How many 3/4s fit in 3?

Dividing fractions answers two kinds of questions: how many pieces of one size fit in an amount, and how much goes in one group. With Lumi you will count how many servings fit, share amounts equally, find the missing side of a rectangle, multiply by the reciprocal, check your answers with multiplication, predict whether the answer is bigger or smaller, and decide who is right. With Israel, Daniel, Elías, Sophia, Athena, Salma and Selena.

Dividir fracciones responde dos tipos de preguntas: cuántas piezas de un tamaño caben en una cantidad y cuánto le toca a un grupo. Con Lumi vas a contar cuántas porciones caben, repartir cantidades en partes iguales, hallar el lado que falta de un rectángulo, multiplicar por el recíproco, comprobar tus respuestas con la multiplicación, anticipar si la respuesta es mayor o menor y decidir quién tiene razón.

Part 1 · Explain

Daniel has 2 1/4 cups of trail mix and fills bags with 3/4 cup each. Salma says: “He can fill 3 bags.” Elías says: “He can fill 1 11/16 bags, because 2 1/4 × 3/4 = 1 11/16.” Who is right? Explain with a model and with an equation.

Daniel tiene 2 1/4 tazas de mezcla de frutos secos y llena bolsas de 3/4 de taza cada una. Salma dice: «Puede llenar 3 bolsas». Elías dice: «Puede llenar 1 11/16 bolsas, porque 2 1/4 × 3/4 = 1 11/16». ¿Quién tiene razón? Explica con un modelo y con una ecuación.

Sentence starters: The question asks how many ___ fit in ___, so the equation is ___ ÷ ___. In fourths, 2 1/4 is ___ fourths and each bag is ___ fourths, so ___. Elías ___ instead of dividing.

Useful words: divide, how many fit, fourths, model, equation, quotient, multiply.

Challenge: how many bags could Daniel fill with 1/2 cup each? What does the fraction in your answer mean?

Reto: ¿cuántas bolsas de 1/2 taza podría llenar Daniel? ¿Qué significa la fracción de tu respuesta?

Part 2 · Apply the idea to a new problem

A rectangular mural has an area of 3/4 square yard. It is 1/2 yard tall. How long is it? Write the equation, solve it and check your answer with multiplication. Then write your own story for 3/4 ÷ 1/2.

Un mural rectangular tiene un área de 3/4 de yarda cuadrada. Mide 1/2 yarda de alto. ¿Cuánto mide de largo? Escribe la ecuación, resuélvela y comprueba tu respuesta con la multiplicación. Después escribe tu propia historia para 3/4 ÷ 1/2.

Sentence starters: Area = length × height, so 1/2 × ___ = 3/4. The length is 3/4 ÷ 1/2 = ___. Check: ___ × 1/2 = ___. My story: ___.

Challenge: write two different stories for 2/3 ÷ 1/4, one that asks “how many fit?” and one that asks “how much in one whole group?”, and solve them.

Reto: escribe dos historias distintas para 2/3 ÷ 1/4, una que pregunte «¿cuántas caben?» y otra que pregunte «¿cuánto hay en un grupo entero?», y resuélvelas.

Check your own work

Read your answers again. Press each sentence that is true about your work.

Vuelve a leer tus respuestas. Pulsa cada oración que sea verdadera sobre tu trabajo.

Part 1. Salma is right. The question is how many 3/4-cup bags fit in 2 1/4 cups, so the equation is 2 1/4 ÷ 3/4. In fourths, 2 1/4 cups is 9 fourths and each bag is 3 fourths, so 9 ÷ 3 = 3 bags. Check: 3 × 3/4 = 9/4 = 2 1/4. Elías multiplied instead of dividing; his answer is less than 2 1/4, but more than two bags clearly fit.

Part 2. The length is 1 1/2 yards. Area = length × height, so 1/2 × ? = 3/4, and ? = 3/4 ÷ 1/2 = 3/4 × 2 = 6/4 = 1 1/2. Check: 1 1/2 × 1/2 = 3/4. A story: “I have 3/4 of a pound of clay and each figure uses 1/2 pound. How many figures can I make?” The answer is 1 1/2 figures.

Challenge. With 1/2-cup bags: 2 1/4 ÷ 1/2 = 4 1/2. Daniel fills 4 bags, and the 1/2 means half of a bag: the 1/4 cup left over is half of a 1/2-cup bag. For 2/3 ÷ 1/4 = 2 2/3: “How many 1/4-mile laps fit in a 2/3-mile path?” (2 2/3 laps) and “1/4 of a tank holds 2/3 of a gallon. How much does the whole tank hold?” (2 2/3 gallons).

Your words can be different. What matters is that your answer shows the math and explains how it proves your answer.

Aligned to the Illinois sixth grade mathematics standard 6.NS.1: interpret and compute quotients of fractions, and solve word problems involving division of fractions by fractions, using visual fraction models and equations to represent the problem. For example, create a story context for 2/3 ÷ 3/4 and use a model to show the quotient; use the relationship between multiplication and division to explain that 2/3 ÷ 3/4 = 8/9 because 3/4 of 8/9 is 2/3; find how much chocolate each person gets if 3 people share 1/2 pound equally, how many 3/4-cup servings are in 2/3 of a cup of yogurt, and how wide a rectangle is with length 3/4 mile and area 1/2 square mile. The games work with both meanings of division: “how many fit?” and “how much in one group?”. At the Easy level a number line shows the jumps, a bar shows the shares, and the reciprocal is chosen before computing; at the Challenge level the answers can include part of a group, the child finds the whole from a part and the missing side of a rectangle, computes with the reciprocal, checks claimed answers with multiplication, and in “Who is right?” must also choose the reason. Two typical mistakes get their own hints: multiplying instead of dividing, and naming the leftover amount instead of the fraction of a group. The optional “Ayuda en español” button translates only the instruction or the question; the game itself stays in English. Each game has its own direct link: #cuantas, #compartir, #reciproco, #comprobar, #estimar and #quien, and the final task (build and explain an answer without options) is #tarea.

Alineado con el estándar de matemáticas de Illinois para sexto grado 6.NS.1: interpretar y calcular cocientes de fracciones y resolver problemas de división de fracciones entre fracciones con modelos visuales y ecuaciones. Los juegos trabajan los dos significados de la división: «¿cuántas caben?» y «¿cuánto hay en un grupo?». En el nivel Fácil una recta numérica muestra los saltos y una barra muestra las partes; en el Reto las respuestas pueden incluir parte de un grupo, se halla el total a partir de una parte y el lado que falta de un rectángulo, se calcula con el recíproco y se comprueban resultados con la multiplicación. Dos errores típicos tienen su propia pista: multiplicar en lugar de dividir y dar la cantidad sobrante en lugar de la fracción de un grupo. Desde quinto grado los juegos son en inglés; el botón «Ayuda en español» traduce solo la consigna o la pregunta.
