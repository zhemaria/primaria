Final task: explain it yourself

Esta vez no hay opciones para elegir. Lee el artículo, escribe su idea central y un resumen objetivo, y explica cómo desarrolla el autor las ideas.

The article: Small Shells, Big Trouble

1In 1988, scientists found a tiny striped mussel in Lake St. Clair, between Michigan and Canada. It was the zebra mussel, a shellfish about the size of a fingernail that came from Eastern Europe. It had probably arrived in the ballast water of a ship, water that ships carry for balance and then pump out when they reach port.

2Zebra mussels spread quickly through the Great Lakes, including Lake Michigan. A single female can release hundreds of thousands of eggs a year, and the young mussels attach to almost any hard surface.

3Because they grow in thick layers, zebra mussels can clog the pipes that cities and power plants use to take in water. Cleaning these pipes costs millions of dollars.

4The mussels also change the lake itself. Each one filters water to eat tiny floating plants and animals called plankton. As a result, the water in parts of the lakes has become much clearer. That may sound like good news, but clear water can mean less food for young fish and other animals that depend on plankton.

5Today, an even more common relative, the quagga mussel, covers much of the bottom of Lake Michigan. To slow the spread, boaters are asked to clean, drain and dry their boats before moving them to another lake.

Part 1 · Central idea and objective summary

Write the central idea of the article in one sentence. Then write an objective summary in three or four sentences, without opinions.

Escribe la idea central del artículo en una oración. Después escribe un resumen objetivo de tres o cuatro oraciones, sin opiniones.

Sentence starters: The central idea is that ___. Summary: In 1988, ___. Zebra mussels ___. They also ___. To slow the spread, ___.

Words to avoid in a summary: I think, the best, the worst, terrible, should.

Challenge: also name the two most important details that support the central idea, and explain why you chose them.

Reto: nombra también los dos detalles más importantes que apoyan la idea central y explica por qué los elegiste.

Part 2 · How the author develops the ideas

The author wants to show that zebra mussels cause serious problems. Choose two ways the author develops this idea (for example, numbers, causes and effects, a definition or a surprising contrast). For each one, quote the sentence and explain how it helps.

El autor quiere mostrar que los mejillones cebra causan problemas serios. Elige dos maneras en que el autor desarrolla esta idea (por ejemplo, números, causas y efectos, una definición o un contraste sorprendente). En cada una, cita la oración y explica cómo ayuda.

Sentence starters: First, the author uses ___: “___.” This helps because ___. Second, the author ___: “___.” This shows ___.

Challenge: explain how paragraph 5 fits into the structure of the article.

Reto: explica cómo encaja el párrafo 5 en la estructura del artículo.

Part 1. Central idea: Zebra mussels arrived in the Great Lakes by accident, spread quickly and caused serious problems for people and for the lakes. Summary: In 1988, scientists found zebra mussels from Eastern Europe in the Great Lakes, probably carried there in ships' ballast water. The mussels spread quickly and clog the pipes that cities use to take in water. They also eat plankton, which makes the water clearer but leaves less food for young fish. To slow the spread, boaters are asked to clean, drain and dry their boats.

Challenge. The two most important details are that the mussels clog water pipes, which costs millions of dollars, and that they leave less food for young fish. I chose them because they show the two kinds of problems: problems for people and problems for the lake.

Part 2. First, the author uses numbers: “Cleaning these pipes costs millions of dollars.” This helps because a big number shows how serious the problem is for cities. Second, the author uses a surprising contrast: “That may sound like good news, but clear water can mean less food for young fish and other animals that depend on plankton.” This shows that even a change that looks good can hurt the lake.

Challenge. Paragraph 5 comes after the problems. It shows that the problem continues today with the quagga mussel and gives a solution: clean, drain and dry boats.

Your words can be different. What matters is that your summary is objective and that you quoted and explained the author's techniques.
