Words can whisper, shout and feel!

Thrifty, economical and stingy all mean careful with money, but they don't feel the same: thrifty sounds wise, and stingy sounds unkind. Writers choose their words for the feeling they carry. With Lumi you will use clues in a sentence to figure out new words, understand personification and other figures of speech, find how words are related, feel the connotation of a word, learn the academic words your teachers use, and decide who is right. With Israel, Daniel, Elías, Sophia, Athena, Salma and Selena.

Thrifty, economical y stingy significan «cuidadoso con el dinero», pero no se sienten igual: thrifty suena sabio y stingy suena poco amable. Los escritores eligen sus palabras por el sentimiento que llevan. Con Lumi vas a usar pistas de la oración para descubrir palabras nuevas, entender la personificación y otras figuras, encontrar cómo se relacionan las palabras, sentir la connotación de una palabra, aprender las palabras académicas que usan tus maestros y decidir quién tiene razón.

Final task: explain it yourself

There are no options to choose from this time. Be the writer: choose words that change the feeling of a sentence, and explain what an author really means with personification.

Esta vez no hay opciones para elegir. Sé tú el escritor: elige palabras que cambien el sentimiento de una oración y explica lo que de verdad quiere decir un autor con una personificación.

Part 1 · Change the feeling

“Daniel's uncle does not spend much money.”

Rewrite this sentence two times. In the first one, make the uncle sound wise and admirable. In the second one, make him sound unkind. Change only a word or two, and explain which word changed the feeling.

Vuelve a escribir esta oración dos veces. En la primera, haz que el tío parezca sabio y admirable. En la segunda, haz que parezca poco amable. Cambia solo una o dos palabras y explica qué palabra cambió el sentimiento.

Sentence starters: Positive: Daniel's uncle is ___. Negative: Daniel's uncle is ___. The word ___ sounds positive because ___, and the word ___ sounds negative because ___.

Useful words: thrifty, economical, stingy, connotation, denotation, positive, negative, neutral.

Challenge: do the same with “Salma's little sister asks a lot of questions,” and add a detail that shows the feeling.

Reto: haz lo mismo con «Salma's little sister asks a lot of questions» y agrega un detalle que muestre el sentimiento.

Part 2 · What does the author really mean?

“When the last bell rang, the old school sighed with relief, and its tired doors yawned open to let the students out.”

What is being personified? What does the sentence really mean? Why do you think the author wrote it this way? Then write your own sentence that personifies something in your home or your neighborhood.

¿Qué se personifica? ¿Qué quiere decir de verdad la oración? ¿Por qué crees que el autor la escribió así? Después escribe tu propia oración que personifique algo de tu casa o de tu barrio.

Sentence starters: The author personifies ___. A school can't really ___, so it means ___. The author wrote it this way to ___. My sentence: ___.

Challenge: in your sentence, use a simile too, and label both.

Reto: en tu oración usa también un símil y señala los dos.

Check your own work

Read your answers again. Press each sentence that is true about your work.

Vuelve a leer tus respuestas. Pulsa cada oración que sea verdadera sobre tu trabajo.

Part 1. Positive: “Daniel's uncle is thrifty: he saves his money carefully.” Negative: “Daniel's uncle is stingy: he never spends his money on anyone.” Both words mean about the same thing, not spending much money (the same denotation). Thrifty sounds wise and careful, but stingy sounds selfish, as if he doesn't want to share (different connotations).

Challenge. Positive: “Salma's curious little sister asks a lot of questions because she loves to learn.” Negative: “Salma's nosy little sister asks a lot of questions about everybody's private business.”

Part 2. The author personifies the school and its doors. A building can't really sigh or yawn, so the sentence means that the school day is over, everything calms down and the doors open wide for the students to leave. The author wrote it this way to show how tired and relieved everyone feels at the end of a long day, as if even the building were tired. My sentence: “Every morning, our old coffee maker grumbles and sputters before it finally wakes up.”

Your words can be different. What matters is that you explained the feeling of each word and what the personification really means.

Your answers are not saved. Show them to your teacher or your family, or use the printed page.

Tus respuestas no se guardan. Muéstraselas a tu maestra o a tu familia, o usa la hoja impresa.

Every wrong answer gets a hint that explains the mistake (for example, confusing a part of something with a kind of something, or taking a figure of speech literally). The final task asks the student to change the connotation of a sentence and to explain and write personification. Context clues: definition (which means, or, :) · example (such as, including) · contrast (but, however, unlike) · cause and effect (so, because, as a result)

Figurative language: personification (human actions for things that are not human) · simile (like, as) · metaphor (one thing IS another) · hyperbole (a huge exaggeration)

a) Daniel's dog is usually docile, but today it barked and jumped at every person who walked by.

b) Many invertebrates live in Lake Michigan, including mussels, crayfish and tiny shrimp.
