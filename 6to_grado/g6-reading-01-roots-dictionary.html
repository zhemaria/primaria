Words have parts, and dictionaries have secrets!

Aud means hear. So an audience hears a show, an auditory learner learns by listening, and a sound that is audible can be heard. With Lumi you will learn sixth grade roots like bene, mal, duct, flect and path, sort word families, see how a suffix can turn a word into a verb, a noun or an adjective, build words from their parts, check the meaning of a new word in its sentence, and read a dictionary entry to find the right meaning and the right way to say a word. With Israel, Daniel, Elías, Sophia, Athena, Salma and Selena.

Aud significa «oír». Por eso una audience (el público) oye un espectáculo, un auditory learner aprende escuchando y un sonido audible se puede oír. Con Lumi vas a aprender raíces de sexto grado como bene, mal, duct, flect y path, ordenar familias de palabras, ver cómo un sufijo convierte una palabra en verbo, sustantivo o adjetivo, construir palabras con sus partes, comprobar el significado de una palabra nueva en su oración y leer una entrada del diccionario para encontrar el significado correcto y la pronunciación correcta.

Final task: explain it yourself

There are no options to choose from this time. Figure out a new word on your own, the way a good reader does: make a guess with the parts of the word, check it with the sentence, and tell how you would confirm it in a dictionary.

Esta vez no hay opciones para elegir. Descubre tú solo una palabra nueva, como lo hace un buen lector: adivina con las partes de la palabra, compruébalo con la oración y di cómo lo confirmarías en un diccionario.

Part 1 · Figure out a new word

“Mrs. Delgado, Salma's neighbor, is famous on her block for being benevolent: every Saturday she cooks soup for the people at the shelter, and last winter she gave her own coat to a man who had none.”

What does benevolent probably mean? Explain how the root bene and the words in the sentence helped you. Then tell how you would check your guess.

¿Qué significa probablemente benevolent? Explica cómo te ayudaron la raíz bene y las palabras de la oración. Después di cómo comprobarías tu respuesta.

Sentence starters: Benevolent probably means ___. The root bene means ___. The sentence says that she ___, and that shows ___. To check my guess, I would ___.

Useful words: root, prefix, suffix, context clue, confirm, dictionary, entry, part of speech.

Challenge: also say what part of speech benevolent is in this sentence and how you know. Then write the word that means the opposite, using the root mal.

Reto: di también qué clase de palabra es benevolent en esta oración y cómo lo sabes. Después escribe la palabra que significa lo contrario, usando la raíz mal.

Part 2 · Same spelling, two ways to say it

The dictionary shows two ways to say the word object: OB-jekt (a noun: a thing) and ub-JEKT (a verb: to say you disagree). Write one sentence with each one. Then explain how a reader can tell which one you mean.

El diccionario muestra dos maneras de pronunciar la palabra object: OB-jekt (sustantivo: una cosa) y ub-JEKT (verbo: decir que no estás de acuerdo). Escribe una oración con cada una. Después explica cómo sabe el lector cuál de las dos quieres decir.

Sentence starters: In my first sentence, object is a noun because ___. In my second sentence, it is a verb because ___. A reader can tell by ___.

Challenge: do the same with another word from the game: record, present, desert, permit or refuse.

Reto: haz lo mismo con otra palabra del juego: record, present, desert, permit o refuse.

Check your own work

Read your answers again. Press each sentence that is true about your work.

Vuelve a leer tus respuestas. Pulsa cada oración que sea verdadera sobre tu trabajo.

Part 1. Benevolent probably means kind and wanting to do good for others. The root bene means good or well. The sentence confirms it: Mrs. Delgado “cooks soup for the people at the shelter” and “gave her own coat to a man who had none.” Those are good, generous actions. To check my guess, I would look up benevolent in a dictionary and read the meaning that fits a person.

Challenge. Benevolent is an adjective: it describes Mrs. Delgado, after “for being.” The opposite is malevolent: wanting to do bad things to others.

Part 2. “Athena found a strange object in the sand: a tiny metal key.” (OB-jekt, a noun: it comes after “a strange” and names a thing.) “I object to the new rule, because it is not fair for the younger kids.” (ub-JEKT, a verb: it comes after “I” and tells what I do.) A reader can tell by the job the word does in the sentence: a noun names a thing, and a verb is an action.

Your words can be different. What matters is that you used the root, quoted the sentence and explained how you would check the meaning.

Aligned to the Illinois sixth grade English Language Arts standards L.6.4a (use context, such as the overall meaning of a sentence or paragraph or a word's position or function in a sentence, as a clue to the meaning of a word), L.6.4b (use common, grade-appropriate Greek or Latin affixes and roots as clues to the meaning of a word, e.g., audience, auditory, audible), L.6.4c (consult reference materials, both print and digital, to find the pronunciation of a word or determine or clarify its precise meaning or its part of speech) and L.6.4d (verify the preliminary determination of the meaning of a word or phrase, e.g., by checking the inferred meaning in context or in a dictionary). At the Easy level the root is highlighted, the word parts are colored, a key shows what each part means, the dictionary entry is in view from the start and, after a mistake, the clue in the sentence is marked; at the Challenge level those supports are removed and the student must prove the answer: name the part of speech the suffix creates, choose the words in the sentence that confirm a first guess, and decide the part of speech before looking up the meaning and the pronunciation (record: REK-erd as a noun, rih-KORD as a verb). Every wrong answer gets a hint that explains the mistake (for example, mixing up bene “good” and mal “bad,” reversing a prefix, or choosing a meaning that belongs to another part of speech). The final task asks the student to figure out a new word without options and explain how the parts and the context helped. The optional “Ayuda en español” button translates only the instruction or the question, never the words, the sentences or the answers, so the game stays in English (WIDA-friendly support for English learners).
