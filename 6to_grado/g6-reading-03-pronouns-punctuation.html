Pronouns and punctuation

👥💪✒️🎨

Clear pronouns, clean punctuation!

“Daniel told Elías that he won.” Who won? Pronouns are small words with a big job: each one must point clearly to one person or thing. With Lumi you will choose between I and me, use pronouns like herself and themselves, fix pronouns that suddenly shift, make unclear pronouns clear, set off extra information with commas, parentheses and dashes, and turn choppy sentences into smooth ones. With Israel, Daniel, Elías, Sophia, Athena, Salma and Selena.

«Daniel told Elías that he won.» ¿Quién ganó? Los pronombres son palabras pequeñas con un gran trabajo: cada uno debe referirse con claridad a una sola persona o cosa. Con Lumi vas a elegir entre I y me, usar pronombres como herself y themselves, corregir pronombres que cambian de repente, aclarar pronombres confusos, separar la información extra con comas, paréntesis y rayas, y convertir oraciones cortadas en oraciones fluidas.

Final task: explain it yourself

There are no options to choose from this time. Be the editor: fix the pronouns in a short paragraph and explain your changes. Then write your own sentences with extra information.

Esta vez no hay opciones para elegir. Sé tú el editor: corrige los pronombres de un párrafo corto y explica tus cambios. Después escribe tus propias oraciones con información extra.

Part 1 · Edit the paragraph

“Elías and me went to the Field Museum with our class. When students visit the museum, you can see Sue, the T. rex. Elías told Daniel that he took the best photo. The guide gave Daniel and I a map, and we found the mummies ourself.”

Rewrite the paragraph without pronoun mistakes. Then explain at least two of your changes.

Vuelve a escribir el párrafo sin errores de pronombres. Después explica por lo menos dos de tus cambios.

Sentence starters: I changed ___ to ___ because ___. The pronoun “he” was unclear because ___, so I wrote ___.

Useful words: subject pronoun, object pronoun, shift, antecedent, unclear, reflexive, intensive.

Challenge: there are five mistakes. Find all five and name the kind of mistake for each one.

Reto: hay cinco errores. Encuéntralos todos y di de qué tipo es cada uno.

Part 2 · Add extra information

Write three sentences about a person or a place you know. Add extra information to each one: use commas in the first, parentheses in the second and dashes in the third. Then explain how you know the information is extra.

Escribe tres oraciones sobre una persona o un lugar que conozcas. Agrega información extra a cada una: usa comas en la primera, paréntesis en la segunda y rayas en la tercera. Después explica cómo sabes que esa información es extra.

Example: My grandmother, who was born in Mayagüez, makes the best flan.

Sentence starter: I know the information is extra because ___.

Challenge: write a fourth sentence that combines two short sentences in a new way (begin with when, because or although).

Reto: escribe una cuarta oración que combine dos oraciones cortas de una manera nueva (empieza con when, because o although).

Check your own work

Read your answers again. Press each sentence that is true about your work.

Vuelve a leer tus respuestas. Pulsa cada oración que sea verdadera sobre tu trabajo.

Part 1. “Elías and I went to the Field Museum with our class. When students visit the museum, they can see Sue, the T. rex. Elías told Daniel, ‘I took the best photo!’ The guide gave Daniel and me a map, and we found the mummies ourselves.” I changed “Elías and me went” to “Elías and I went” because the pronoun is the subject: “I went.” I changed “you can see” to “they can see” because the sentence is about students, so the pronoun shouldn't shift to you.

Challenge. The five mistakes: me → I (case: subject); you → they (shift in person); he → a quote that shows who took the photo (vague pronoun); I → me (case: object after gave); ourself → ourselves (we is plural; this intensive pronoun adds emphasis).

Part 2. “My grandmother, who was born in Mayagüez, makes the best flan.” “Garfield Park Conservatory (it is free on most days) has a room full of giant ferns.” “My little brother—the loudest person I know—fell asleep during the fireworks.” I know the information is extra because if I take it out, each sentence still makes sense.

Challenge. “Although it was raining, we walked to the conservatory.”

Your sentences can be different. What matters is that every pronoun is correct and clear, and that the extra information has marks on both sides.

Your answers are not saved. Show them to your teacher or your family, or use the printed page.

Tus respuestas no se guardan. Muéstraselas a tu maestra o a tu familia, o usa la hoja impresa.

Aligned to the Illinois sixth grade English Language Arts standards L.6.1a (ensure that pronouns are in the proper case: subjective, objective, possessive), L.6.1b (use intensive pronouns, e.g., myself, ourselves), L.6.1c (recognize and correct inappropriate shifts in pronoun number and person), L.6.1d (recognize and correct vague pronouns, i.e., ones with unclear or ambiguous antecedents), L.6.1e (recognize variations from standard English in their own and others' writing and identify and use strategies to improve expression in conventional language), L.6.2a (use punctuation, commas, parentheses, dashes, to set off nonrestrictive or parenthetical elements) and L.6.3a (vary sentence patterns for meaning, reader or listener interest, and style). At the Easy level the pronoun to fix is marked, the unclear pronoun is explained and the extra information is named; at the Challenge level the student must find the mistake first and then prove the answer: name the case of the pronoun, decide whether a pronoun is intensive or reflexive by taking it out, point to the extra information and name what the writer did to combine sentences. Every wrong answer gets a hint that explains the mistake (for example, “cover Daniel and: me went sounds wrong”). Non-standard forms such as hisself and theirselves appear only as wrong options with an explanation (L.6.1e). The final task asks the student to edit a paragraph and explain the changes, and to write sentences with extra information. The optional “Ayuda en español” button translates only the instruction or the question, never the sentences or the answers, so the game stays in English (WIDA-friendly support for English learners). 1. Circle the correct pronoun.

a) Daniel and (I / me) went to the game. b) Coach gave Selena and (I / me) new jerseys. c) Between you and (I / me), I loved it. d) The dog wagged (its / it's) tail.

2. Fix the pronoun shift. Write the correct pronoun.

a) When students practice every day, you get better. ________ b) The players thanked his fans. ________

3. Rewrite the sentence so the pronoun is clear.

Sophia put the cake on the table, and then it fell.

4. Add the missing marks around the extra information.

a) (commas) The Bean a giant shiny sculpture is in Millennium Park.

b) (parentheses) The Great Chicago Fire 1871 destroyed a third of the city.

c) (dashes) The best part of the trip and everyone agreed was the boat ride.

5. Combine into one smooth sentence: The bell rang. The students ran to the buses.
