Part 1 · Explain

Athena says: “3(x + 4) and 3x + 4 are equivalent, because they have the same numbers and the same letter.” Is she right? Explain with the distributive property, and check by putting x = 2 into both expressions.

Athena dice: «3(x + 4) y 3x + 4 son equivalentes, porque tienen los mismos números y la misma letra». ¿Tiene razón? Explica con la propiedad distributiva y comprueba poniendo x = 2 en las dos expresiones.

Sentence starters: By the distributive property, 3(x + 4) = 3 × ___ + 3 × ___ = ___. When x = 2, 3(x + 4) = ___ and 3x + 4 = ___. So Athena is ___ because ___.

Useful words: expression, equivalent, distributive property, term, coefficient, evaluate, substitute.

Challenge: one example where two expressions have the same value is not enough to prove they are equivalent. Explain why, and find a value of x that makes 2x + 6 and x + 9 equal even though they are not equivalent.

Reto: un solo ejemplo en el que dos expresiones dan el mismo valor no basta para demostrar que son equivalentes. Explica por qué y halla un valor de x que haga iguales 2x + 6 y x + 9 aunque no sean equivalentes.

Part 1. Athena is not right. By the distributive property, 3(x + 4) = 3 × x + 3 × 4 = 3x + 12, not 3x + 4: the 3 multiplies both terms inside the parentheses. Check with x = 2: 3(2 + 4) = 3 × 6 = 18, but 3 × 2 + 4 = 6 + 4 = 10. Since 18 is not 10, the expressions are not equivalent.

Part 2. Both are right. 6t + 2t means 6 t's plus 2 t's, which is 8 t's, so 6t + 2t = 8t; each ticket really costs $6 + $2 = $8. For 15 tickets: 8 × 15 = $120. The volume of a cube is s × s × s = s³; when s = 3, it is 3³ = 27 cubic units.

Challenge. Two different expressions can give the same value for one number by chance, so one example cannot prove they are always equal; a property can. For example, 2x + 6 and x + 9 are both 12 when x = 3, but when x = 0 they are 6 and 9. The GCF of 24 and 18 is 6, so 24x + 18 = 6(4x + 3); by the distributive property, 6 × 4x + 6 × 3 = 24x + 18.

Your words can be different. What matters is that your answer shows the math and explains how it proves your answer.
