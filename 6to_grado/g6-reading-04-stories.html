The story: The Quiet Kid on the 49 Bus

A new kid named Mateo started riding the 49 bus in October. He always sat in the back with his hood up and never said a word.

I decided he was stuck-up. When Daniel said hi to him, Mateo just nodded and looked out the window. For two weeks, we called him "the Statue" when he couldn't hear us.

One rainy morning, Mateo's notebook slid off his lap and landed open at my feet. Every page was covered with comics: superheroes flying over the Chicago skyline, drawn in tiny, perfect lines. One hero looked just like our bus driver, Ms. Jackson, with a cape made of bus transfers.

"You drew these?" I asked. Mateo's ears turned pink. "My English isn't good yet," he said quietly. "Drawing is easier." I felt my face get hot, remembering the nickname.

Now Mateo sits next to me, and I'm teaching him bus slang while he teaches me how to draw hands. He's still quiet, but I've learned that quiet doesn't mean stuck-up.

Part 1 · Theme with evidence

What is the theme of the story? Explain how the author develops it through what Israel thinks and does. Quote at least two pieces of evidence, with quotation marks.

¿Cuál es el tema del cuento? Explica cómo lo desarrolla el autor a través de lo que Israel piensa y hace. Cita por lo menos dos pruebas, entre comillas.

Sentence starters: The theme is ___. At the beginning, Israel ___: “___.” After ___, he ___: “___.” This shows that ___.

Useful words: theme, develop, at first, turning point, evidence, quote, shows, reveals.

Challenge: also write an objective summary of the story in two or three sentences, with no opinions.

Reto: escribe también un resumen objetivo del cuento en dos o tres oraciones, sin opiniones.

Part 2 · Another point of view

Retell the rainy morning from Mateo's point of view, using I, in five or six sentences. Show what Mateo knows and feels that Israel doesn't know.

Vuelve a contar la mañana lluviosa desde el punto de vista de Mateo, usando «I», en cinco o seis oraciones. Muestra lo que Mateo sabe y siente y que Israel no sabe.

Sentence starters: Every morning on the bus, I ___. When my notebook fell, I felt ___. The boy asked, “You drew these?” and I ___. I didn't tell him that ___.

Challenge: after your retelling, explain in one or two sentences how changing the narrator changes what the reader understands.

Reto: después de tu versión, explica en una o dos oraciones cómo cambia lo que entiende el lector cuando cambia el narrador.

Part 1. The theme is that first impressions can be wrong, so we should get to know people before we judge them. At the beginning, Israel judges Mateo only by his silence: “I decided he was stuck-up.” The turning point comes when Mateo's notebook falls open and Israel learns why he is quiet: “My English isn't good yet,” Mateo says. “Drawing is easier.” At the end, Israel sits next to Mateo and admits he was wrong: “quiet doesn't mean stuck-up.” Israel's change from judging to understanding develops the theme.

Challenge. A quiet new student, Mateo, starts riding the 49 bus, and Israel decides he is stuck-up. When Mateo's notebook falls open, Israel sees his comics and learns that Mateo is still learning English. The two boys become friends and teach each other.

Part 2. “Every morning on the 49 bus, I practice English sentences in my head, but they never come out. On that rainy morning, my notebook slid off my lap and fell open at a boy's feet. I wanted to grab it, because my comics are the only place where I can say everything I want. The boy picked it up and asked, ‘You drew these?’ My ears got hot, but he was smiling, not laughing. He didn't know that I had wanted to say hello to him since my very first day.”

Challenge. When Israel tells the story, the reader also thinks Mateo is stuck-up at first. When Mateo tells it, the reader knows from the start that he is nervous, not stuck-up.

Aligned to the Illinois sixth grade English Language Arts standards RL.6.1 (cite textual evidence to support analysis of what the text says explicitly as well as inferences drawn from the text), RL.6.2 (determine a theme or central idea of a text and how it is conveyed through particular details; provide a summary of the text distinct from personal opinions or judgments), RL.6.3 (describe how a particular story's or drama's plot unfolds in a series of episodes as well as how the characters respond or change as the plot moves toward a resolution), RL.6.4 (determine the meaning of words and phrases as they are used in a text, including figurative and connotative meanings; analyze the impact of a specific word choice on meaning and tone), RL.6.5 (analyze how a particular sentence, chapter, scene, or stanza fits into the overall structure of a text and contributes to the development of the theme, setting, or plot) and RL.6.6 (explain how an author develops the point of view of the narrator or speaker in a text). The texts include four stories, a Greek myth, a poem and a short play. At the Easy level Lumi reads the text aloud while the sentence is highlighted, there are fewer options and the evidence is marked after a mistake; at the Challenge level the texts are longer and the student must prove each answer by choosing the sentence, line or stanza that supports it, sort all the details about how a character changes and explain what caused the change. Every wrong answer gets a hint that explains the mistake (for example, confusing the topic with the theme, or a summary that includes an opinion). The final task asks the student to explain a theme with exact quotations and to retell a scene from another character's point of view. The optional “Ayuda en español” button translates only the instruction or the question, never the text or the answers, so the reading stays in English (WIDA-friendly support for English learners). Each game has its own direct link: #escucha, #citar, #tema, #comparar, #estructura, #narrador and #tarea.

Alineado con los estándares de Artes del Lenguaje en Inglés de Illinois para sexto grado: RL.6.1 (citar evidencia del texto para apoyar el análisis de lo que dice de forma explícita y de las inferencias), RL.6.2 (determinar el tema o la idea central y cómo se transmite con detalles; resumir el texto sin opiniones personales), RL.6.3 (describir cómo se desarrolla la trama en episodios y cómo responden o cambian los personajes hacia la resolución), RL.6.4 (el significado de palabras y frases, incluido el figurado y el connotativo, y el efecto de la elección de palabras en el significado y el tono), RL.6.5 (cómo encaja una oración, un capítulo, una escena o una estrofa en la estructura y contribuye al tema, al ambiente o a la trama) y RL.6.6 (cómo desarrolla el autor el punto de vista del narrador o del hablante). Los juegos son en inglés; el botón «Ayuda en español» traduce solo la consigna o la pregunta.
