Part 1. Athena found the greatest number. The factors of 48 are 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 16, 24, 48, and the factors of 36 are 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 9, 12, 18, 36. The common factors are 1, 2, 3, 4, 6 and 12, and the greatest is 12. So Salma can make 12 bags, each with 48 ÷ 12 = 4 granola bars and 36 ÷ 12 = 3 apples. She can't make more bags, because no number greater than 12 divides both 48 and 36 evenly.

Part 2. Multiples of 6: 6, 12, 18, 24, 30… Multiples of 8: 8, 16, 24, 32… The least common multiple is 24, so the lights blink together again after 24 seconds. The GCF of 42 and 56 is 14, so 42 + 56 = 14 × 3 + 14 × 4 = 14 × (3 + 4) = 14 × 7 = 98. Inside the parentheses, 3 and 4 have no common factor other than 1.

Challenge. 6 is also a common factor, so 6 bags of 8 bars and 6 apples work, but each bag is bigger and there are fewer bags; the greatest common factor makes the most identical bags. The LCM is not always the product: for 4 and 5 (no common factor) it is 20 = 4 × 5, but for 6 and 8 it is 24, not 48, because they share the factor 2.

Aligned to the Illinois sixth grade mathematics standard 6.NS.4: find the greatest common factor of two whole numbers less than or equal to 100 and the least common multiple of two whole numbers less than or equal to 12, and use the distributive property to express a sum of two whole numbers 1–100 with a common factor as a multiple of a sum of two whole numbers with no common factor (for example, 36 + 8 = 4 × (9 + 2)). At the Easy level the factor pairs appear as rectangles, the lists of factors or multiples are shown with the common ones marked, and the distributive property comes with an area model; at the Challenge level the child works without lists, finds all the factors of a number, solves word problems that need the GCF or the LCM, chooses the expression that uses the greatest common factor, and in “Who is right?” must also choose the reason. Typical mistakes get their own hints: choosing a common factor that is not the greatest, using the product as the LCM when the numbers share a factor, and confusing the GCF with the LCM. The optional “Ayuda en español” button translates only the instruction or the question; the game itself stays in English. Each game has its own direct link: #factores, #mcd, #mcm, #situaciones, #distributiva and #quien, and the final task (build and explain an answer without options) is #tarea.

En el nivel Fácil las parejas de factores se ven como rectángulos, las listas de factores o de múltiplos aparecen con los comunes marcados y la propiedad distributiva viene con un modelo de área; en el Reto se trabaja sin listas, se hallan todos los factores, se resuelven problemas que piden el MCD o el MCM y se elige la expresión que usa el máximo común divisor.
