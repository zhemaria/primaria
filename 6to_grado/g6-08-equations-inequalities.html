Part 1 · Explain

Salma saves the same amount of money every week. After 6 weeks she has $87. Elías writes 6 + x = 87 and gets x = 81. Athena writes 6x = 87 and gets x = 14.50. Who wrote the right equation? Explain what x means, and check the answer by substitution.

Salma ahorra la misma cantidad de dinero cada semana. Después de 6 semanas tiene $87. Elías escribe 6 + x = 87 y obtiene x = 81. Athena escribe 6x = 87 y obtiene x = 14.50. ¿Quién escribió la ecuación correcta? Explica qué significa x y comprueba la respuesta sustituyéndola.

Sentence starters: x means ___. Salma saves x dollars 6 times, so the equation is ___. To solve it, I ___ both sides by ___, so x = ___. Check: ___. Elías's equation means ___.

Useful words: variable, equation, solution, substitute, inverse operation, both sides, check.

Challenge: write a different story that really matches Elías's equation 6 + x = 87.

Reto: escribe otra historia que de verdad corresponda a la ecuación de Elías, 6 + x = 87.

Part 2 · Apply the idea to a new problem

A school elevator can carry at most 1,500 pounds. Write an inequality for the weight w it can carry, describe how to show it on a number line, and say whether 1,500 pounds and 1,600 pounds are solutions. Then a ride says riders must be taller than 48 inches: write that inequality too, and explain the difference between the two.

El ascensor de la escuela puede llevar como máximo 1,500 libras. Escribe una desigualdad para el peso w que puede llevar, describe cómo mostrarla en una recta numérica y di si 1,500 y 1,600 libras son soluciones. Después, una atracción dice que los usuarios deben medir más de 48 pulgadas: escribe también esa desigualdad y explica la diferencia entre las dos.

Sentence starters: “At most” means ___, so w ___ 1,500. On the number line, the circle at 1,500 is ___ and the arrow points ___. 1,500 is ___ and 1,600 is ___. “Taller than” means ___, so h ___ 48, with an ___ circle.

Challenge: why does an inequality like x > 4.5 have infinitely many solutions? Give three solutions that are not whole numbers.

Reto: ¿por qué una desigualdad como x > 4.5 tiene infinitas soluciones? Da tres soluciones que no sean números enteros.

Part 1. Athena wrote the right equation. x is the money Salma saves each week. She saves x dollars 6 times, so 6x = 87. To undo multiplying by 6, divide both sides by 6: x = 87 ÷ 6 = 14.50. Check: 6 × 14.50 = 87. Elías's equation 6 + x = 87 means “6 plus some amount is 87”, which would be one deposit of $81, not 6 equal weeks.

Part 2. “At most” means less than or equal to, so w ≤ 1,500. On the number line, put a closed (filled) circle at 1,500 and an arrow to the left. 1,500 pounds is a solution (it is equal to the limit), and 1,600 is not. “Taller than 48” means h > 48: an open circle at 48 and an arrow to the right, because exactly 48 inches is not enough.

Challenge. A story for 6 + x = 87: “Elías had $6 and his grandmother gave him some money; now he has $87.” For x > 4.5, every number to the right of 4.5 works, and between any two numbers there are always more numbers, so there are infinitely many solutions: for example 4.6, 5 1/4 and 9.75.

Your words can be different. What matters is that your answer shows the math and explains how it proves your answer.

Aligned to the Illinois sixth grade mathematics standards 6.EE.5 (solving an equation or inequality is answering which values make it true; use substitution to decide whether a number makes it true), 6.EE.6 (use variables to represent numbers and write expressions for real-world problems; a variable can stand for an unknown number or for any number in a set), 6.EE.7 (solve real-world and mathematical problems by writing and solving equations of the form x + p = q and px = q, where p, q and x are nonnegative rational numbers) and 6.EE.8 (write an inequality of the form x > c or x < c for a constraint, recognize that such inequalities have infinitely many solutions, and represent them on number line diagrams). At the Easy level a balance shows the equation, the numbers are whole and the inequality graphs come with their words; at the Challenge level there are decimals and fractions, stories to solve, inequality words such as “at least” and “at most”, and in “Who is right?” the child must also choose the reason. Typical mistakes get their own hints: doing the same operation instead of the inverse, solving only one side, and confusing open and closed circles. The optional “Ayuda en español” button translates only the instruction or the question; the game itself stays in English. Each game has its own direct link: #verdad, #balanza, #multiplica, #plantear, #desigualdad and #quien, and the final task (build and explain an answer without options) is #tarea.

Alineado con los estándares de matemáticas de Illinois para sexto grado 6.EE.5 (resolver una ecuación o desigualdad es hallar qué valores la hacen verdadera; usar la sustitución para comprobarlo), 6.EE.6 (usar variables para representar números en problemas reales), 6.EE.7 (resolver problemas escribiendo y resolviendo ecuaciones de la forma x + p = q y px = q con números racionales no negativos) y 6.EE.8 (escribir una desigualdad x > c o x < c para una condición, reconocer que tiene infinitas soluciones y representarla en la recta numérica). En el nivel Fácil una balanza muestra la ecuación y los números son enteros; en el Reto hay decimales y fracciones, historias para resolver y palabras como «por lo menos» y «como máximo». Los errores típicos tienen su propia pista: hacer la misma operación en lugar de la inversa, resolver solo un lado y confundir el círculo abierto con el cerrado. Desde quinto grado los juegos son en inglés; el botón «Ayuda en español» traduce solo la consigna o la pregunta.
