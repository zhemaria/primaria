Part 1 · Explain

Selena buys 3.5 pounds of apples at $2.40 per pound and pays with a $10 bill. Elías says: “3.5 × 2.40 = 0.84, so her change is $9.16.” Salma says: “Her change is $1.60.” Who is right? Explain with an estimate and with the calculation.

Selena compra 3.5 libras de manzanas a $2.40 la libra y paga con un billete de $10. Elías dice: «3.5 × 2.40 = 0.84, así que le devuelven $9.16». Salma dice: «Le devuelven $1.60». ¿Quién tiene razón? Explica con una estimación y con el cálculo.

Sentence starters: 3.5 × 2.40 is about ___ × ___ = ___, so the apples cost about $___. The exact cost is ___. The change is $10 − ___ = ___. Elías ___.

Useful words: estimate, decimal point, decimal places, product, difference, change, reasonable.

Challenge: how many pounds of apples could Selena buy with the whole $10? Give the answer to the hundredths and explain what the decimal part means.

Reto: ¿cuántas libras de manzanas podría comprar Selena con los $10 completos? Da la respuesta hasta los centésimos y explica qué significa la parte decimal.

Part 2 · Apply the idea to a new problem

The sixth grade raised $1,248 for a field trip, and 32 students are going. How much money is there for each student? Show the long division and check it with multiplication. Then: museum tickets cost $12.75 each. How many tickets can $100 buy, and how much money is left?

Sexto grado recaudó $1,248 para una excursión y van 32 estudiantes. ¿Cuánto dinero hay para cada estudiante? Muestra la división larga y compruébala con la multiplicación. Después: las entradas al museo cuestan $12.75 cada una. ¿Cuántas entradas se pueden comprar con $100 y cuánto dinero sobra?

Sentence starters: 1,248 ÷ 32 is about 1,200 ÷ 30 = ___. The exact answer is ___. Check: ___ × 32 = ___. With $100 you can buy ___ tickets, because ___ × $12.75 = ___, and $___ is left.

Challenge: write your own problem where the remainder of a division must be rounded up (for example, buses for a trip), and solve it.

Check your own work

Read your answers again. Press each sentence that is true about your work.

Vuelve a leer tus respuestas. Pulsa cada oración que sea verdadera sobre tu trabajo.

Part 1. Salma is right. Estimate: 3.5 × 2.40 is about 3.5 × 2 = 7, or 4 × 2.5 = 10, so the apples cost between $7 and $10, not 84 cents. Exact: 3.5 × 2.4 (the same as 2.40): 35 × 24 = 840, and 3.5 and 2.4 have one decimal place each, so the product has two: 8.40. The change is $10.00 − $8.40 = $1.60. Elías put the decimal point in the wrong place: 0.84 is far from the estimate.

Part 2. 1,248 ÷ 32 is about 1,200 ÷ 30 = 40. The long division gives 39: 32 goes into 124 three times (96, remainder 28), bring down the 8 to make 288, and 32 goes into 288 nine times exactly. Check: 39 × 32 = 1,248. Each student has $39. With $100: 7 tickets cost 7 × $12.75 = $89.25, and 8 tickets would cost $102.00, which is too much. So $100 buys 7 tickets and $100 − $89.25 = $10.75 is left.

Challenge. $10 ÷ $2.40 = 4.1666…, so about 4.17 pounds: 4 whole pounds and a little more than one tenth of another pound. A problem that rounds up: “150 students go on a trip and each bus holds 48. 150 ÷ 48 = 3 R 6, so they need 4 buses, because 6 students still need a seat.”

Your words can be different. What matters is that your answer shows the math and explains how it proves your answer.

Aligned to the Illinois sixth grade mathematics standards 6.NS.2 (fluently divide multi-digit numbers using the standard algorithm) and 6.NS.3 (fluently add, subtract, multiply and divide multi-digit decimals using the standard algorithm for each operation). The long division game shows every step in the usual layout and asks for the next digit of the quotient, so the child sees why each digit is chosen. Every calculation is checked against an estimate. At the Easy level the steps already done are shown, the decimals are written in columns with the points lined up, and the child decides where the decimal point goes or which division has the same quotient; at the Challenge level the child finds whole quotients, interprets remainders, judges whether an answer is reasonable, computes products and quotients of decimals, and in “Who is right?” must also choose the reason. Typical mistakes get their own hints: a missing 0 in the quotient, lining up the last digits instead of the decimal points, putting the decimal point in the wrong place, and multiplying the whole numbers and the decimals separately. The optional “Ayuda en español” button translates only the instruction or the question; the game itself stays in English. Each game has its own direct link: #dividir, #estimar, #sumar, #multiplicar, #decimales and #quien, and the final task (build and explain an answer without options) is #tarea.

Alineado con los estándares de matemáticas de Illinois para sexto grado 6.NS.2 (dividir con fluidez números de varias cifras con el algoritmo usual) y 6.NS.3 (sumar, restar, multiplicar y dividir con fluidez decimales de varias cifras con el algoritmo usual de cada operación). El juego de la división larga muestra cada paso y pide la siguiente cifra del cociente. Cada cálculo se contrasta con una estimación. En el nivel Fácil se ven los pasos hechos y los decimales en columnas con los puntos alineados; en el Reto se hallan cocientes completos, se interpretan residuos, se juzga si una respuesta es razonable y se calculan productos y cocientes de decimales. Los errores típicos tienen su propia pista: el 0 que falta en el cociente, alinear las últimas cifras en lugar de los puntos, poner el punto decimal en otro lugar y multiplicar por separado los enteros y los decimales. Desde quinto grado los juegos son en inglés; el botón «Ayuda en español» traduce solo la consigna o la pregunta.
