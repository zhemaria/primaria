The article: The River That Runs Backward

1Picture Chicago in the 1880s: a fast-growing city where factories, stockyards and homes dumped their waste into the Chicago River. The river flowed into Lake Michigan. The problem was that the lake was also where the city got its drinking water.

2Dirty water can carry deadly diseases, such as typhoid fever and cholera. Every heavy rain washed more sewage into the lake, and city leaders knew that something had to change.

3Their answer sounded impossible: make the river run backward. In 1892, workers began digging the Chicago Sanitary and Ship Canal, a channel 28 miles long from the South Branch of the river to Lockport. As many as 8,700 people worked on it at the same time, using tons of dynamite and enormous machines, some of them invented just for this project.

4The canal was finished in January 1900, and the city did not wait for the official ceremony. Leaders had heard that the state of Missouri wanted a court to stop the project, because the waste would now flow toward the Mississippi River and the city of St. Louis. So, early on January 2, a small group quietly opened a cut that let the river water pour into the canal.

5Missouri took Illinois to the U.S. Supreme Court, but in 1906 the court decided in favor of Illinois. In 1999, the American Society of Civil Engineers named the system a Civil Engineering Monument of the Millennium. Today, the canal still carries barges, but scientists also worry that it can carry invasive fish, such as Asian carp, between the Great Lakes and the Mississippi.

Part 1 · Central ideas and objective summary

This article has two central ideas: one about the problem and its solution, and one about the conflict that followed. Write them in one or two sentences. Then write an objective summary in three or four sentences, without opinions.

Este artículo tiene dos ideas centrales: una sobre el problema y su solución, y otra sobre el conflicto que vino después. Escríbelas en una o dos oraciones. Después escribe un resumen objetivo de tres o cuatro oraciones, sin opiniones.

Sentence starters: One central idea is that ___. Another is that ___. Summary: In the 1880s, ___. To solve this, ___. In January 1900, ___. Later, ___.

Words to avoid in a summary: I think, the best, the worst, terrible, should.

Challenge: for each central idea, name the detail that supports it best, and explain why you chose it.

Reto: para cada idea central, nombra el detalle que mejor la apoya y explica por qué lo elegiste.

Part 2 · How events and ideas connect

In this article, one thing leads to another: a problem leads to a plan, the plan leads to a race against time, and the race leads to a court case. Explain this chain of causes and effects. Quote at least two sentences word for word and explain how each one connects to the next event.

En este artículo una cosa lleva a otra: un problema lleva a un plan, el plan lleva a una carrera contra el tiempo y la carrera lleva a un juicio. Explica esta cadena de causas y efectos. Cita al menos dos oraciones palabra por palabra y explica cómo cada una se conecta con el suceso siguiente.

Sentence starters: It started because “___.” This led to ___. Then, “___,” so ___. In the end, ___.

Challenge: the last sentence of the article looks at today. Explain how it changes the way we see the whole story.

Part 1. Central ideas: Chicago reversed its river to protect its drinking water, and this engineering success also brought a conflict with Missouri. Summary: In the 1880s, Chicago’s waste flowed through the river into Lake Michigan, where the city got its drinking water. To solve this, workers spent eight years digging a 28-mile canal that made the river flow away from the lake. In January 1900, Chicago opened the canal early, because Missouri wanted a court to stop it. Missouri lost its case at the Supreme Court in 1906, and the project was later named a Monument of the Millennium.

Challenge. For the first idea: “The problem was that the lake was also where the city got its drinking water.” It shows why the reversal was needed. For the second: “Missouri took Illinois to the U.S. Supreme Court, but in 1906 the court decided in favor of Illinois.” It shows the conflict and how it ended.

Part 2. It started because “The problem was that the lake was also where the city got its drinking water.” This led to the plan to make the river run backward and to the digging of the canal. Then, “Leaders had heard that the state of Missouri wanted a court to stop the project,” so they opened the canal early, before the official ceremony. Because the waste now flowed toward the Mississippi, Missouri took Illinois to court, and the Supreme Court decided in favor of Illinois in 1906.

Challenge. The last sentence shows that the story is not over: the canal that solved one problem may cause a new one, because it can carry invasive fish such as Asian carp. It makes us see the reversal as a success that still has consequences.
