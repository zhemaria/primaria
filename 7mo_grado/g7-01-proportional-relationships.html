Ahmes and the bread of Egypt

About 3,500 years ago, in Egypt, a scribe named Ahmes copied a long roll of papyrus full of math problems. He wrote that he was copying an older text. Today it is called the Rhind Papyrus, after Alexander Henry Rhind, a Scottish collector who bought it in Luxor in 1858. You can see it in the British Museum, in London.

Paid in bread. The Egyptians had no coins. Workers, and also the scribes and officials who supervised them, were paid with rations: bread and beer, often every day, and sometimes grain once a month. So a scribe had to share and trade food fairly, and the papyrus teaches how. Problem 65, for example, shares 100 loaves among 10 men, where three of them (a boatman, a foreman and a doorkeeper) receive a double share. A jar of beer was worth the same as a loaf of bread made from the same flour.

Grain was measured in a unit called the hekat, about 4.8 liters, a little more than a gallon. The pefsu (an ancient Egyptian word; it does not mean weight) of a batch of bread told how many loaves came out of one hekat of grain. Bread of pefsu 10 means 10 loaves for each hekat. A bigger pefsu means more loaves from the same grain, so each loaf is smaller, or weaker.

Nova: So the pefsu is a unit rate: loaves per hekat!

Lumi: Exactly. And loaves = pefsu × hekats. That is a proportional relationship, and the pefsu is its constant of proportionality.

Nova: Then the scribes were doing seventh grade math 3,500 years ago.

Some problems in the papyrus ask the scribe to trade bread of one pefsu for bread of another pefsu made with the same amount of grain. First find the grain, then find the new loaves. Now it is your turn to work like a scribe.

Hace unos 3500 años, en Egipto, un escriba llamado Ahmes copió un largo rollo de papiro lleno de problemas de matemáticas; él mismo escribió que copiaba un texto más antiguo. Hoy se llama papiro de Rhind, por Alexander Henry Rhind, un coleccionista escocés que lo compró en Luxor en 1858, y está en el Museo Británico de Londres. Los egipcios no tenían monedas: a los trabajadores, y también a los escribas y funcionarios que los supervisaban, se les pagaba con raciones de pan y cerveza, a menudo cada día, y a veces con grano una vez al mes. Por eso el escriba tenía que repartir y cambiar alimentos con justicia; el problema 65 del papiro, por ejemplo, reparte 100 panes entre 10 hombres, y tres de ellos (un barquero, un capataz y un portero) reciben ración doble. El grano se medía en hekats (unos 4.8 litros, algo más de un galón). El pefsu (palabra egipcia antigua; no significa «peso») decía cuántos panes salían de un hekat de grano. Un pefsu mayor significa más panes con el mismo grano, es decir, panes más pequeños o más flojos. Nova y Lumi se dan cuenta de que el pefsu es una tasa unitaria (panes por hekat) y la constante de proporcionalidad: panes = pefsu × hekats.

Pefsu is an ancient Egyptian word, and some books write it pesu. It is not related to the Spanish word peso (weight): a bigger pefsu means a smaller loaf, not a heavier one. Ahmes is also written Ahmose. The papyrus is written in hieratic, a fast, handwritten form of Egyptian writing.

Part 1 · Explain

A scribe has 100 loaves of bread of pefsu 10. He must trade them for loaves of pefsu 45, made with the same amount of grain. Daniel says: “He gets 135 loaves, because 45 is 35 more than 10, and 100 + 35 = 135.” Is Daniel right? How many loaves of pefsu 45 does the scribe get?

Un escriba tiene 100 panes de pefsu 10. Debe cambiarlos por panes de pefsu 45 hechos con la misma cantidad de grano. Daniel dice: «Recibe 135 panes, porque 45 es 35 más que 10, y 100 + 35 = 135». ¿Tiene razón Daniel? ¿Cuántos panes de pefsu 45 recibe el escriba?

Sentence starters: Daniel is ___ because ___. First I find the grain: 100 loaves ÷ ___ = ___ hekats. Then ___ hekats × 45 = ___ loaves.

Useful words: pefsu, hekat, unit rate, proportional, constant of proportionality, multiply, divide.

Reto: escribe una ecuación para los panes de pefsu 45, y = kx, donde x son los hekats de grano. ¿Qué significan los puntos (0, 0) y (1, 45) en esta situación?

Part 2 · Apply the idea to a new problem

Selena walks 3/4 of a mile in 1/4 of an hour. Salma says: “Selena walks 3/16 of a mile per hour, because I multiplied 3/4 × 1/4.” Is Salma right? Find Selena’s speed in miles per hour and explain. Then write your own problem like this one.

Selena camina 3/4 de milla en 1/4 de hora. Salma dice: «Selena camina 3/16 de milla por hora, porque multipliqué 3/4 × 1/4». ¿Tiene razón Salma? Halla la velocidad de Selena en millas por hora y explica. Después escribe tu propio problema parecido.

Sentence starters: Salma is ___ because ___. Miles per hour means miles ÷ hours: 3/4 ÷ 1/4 = ___. In a full hour there are ___ quarter hours, so ___.

Challenge: a store adds a 10% tax to a $40 pair of shoes. Israel says the total is $40.10. Is he right? Explain with the percent as a rate per 100.

Reto: una tienda agrega un impuesto del 10% a unos zapatos de $40. Israel dice que el total es $40.10. ¿Tiene razón? Explica con el porcentaje como tasa por cada 100.

Part 1. Daniel is not right. The pefsu is a rate, loaves per hekat, so we must multiply and divide, not add. First the grain: 100 loaves of pefsu 10 use 100 ÷ 10 = 10 hekats. With the same 10 hekats, bread of pefsu 45 gives 10 × 45 = 450 loaves. The scribe gets 450 loaves. They are smaller loaves, because the same grain is shared among more of them.

Challenge. y = 45x. The point (0, 0) means that with 0 hekats of grain you make 0 loaves. The point (1, 45) means that 1 hekat of grain makes 45 loaves: that is the unit rate, the constant of proportionality.

Part 2. Salma is not right. Speed is miles ÷ hours: 3/4 ÷ 1/4 = 3. A full hour has 4 quarter hours, and Selena walks 3/4 of a mile in each one, so in 1 hour she walks 4 × 3/4 = 3 miles. Her speed is 3 miles per hour.

Challenge. Israel is not right. 10% means 10 per 100, or 0.10 of the price: 0.10 × $40 = $4. The total is $40 + $4 = $44 (or 1.10 × $40 = $44).

Your words can be different. What matters is that your answer shows the math and explains how it proves your answer.
