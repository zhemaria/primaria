Math speaks Greek and Latin!

Peri means around and meter means measure, so the perimeter is the measure around a shape. Dia means across, so the diameter is the measure across a circle. Most of the words in your math and science books were built from Greek and Latin parts. With Lumi and Nova you will learn roots like geo, gon, poly, sect, frac, equ, iso, therm, chron, tele and micro, catch impostor words that only look like a root, see how a suffix changes the part of speech, build words from their parts, check a new word in its sentence, and read a dictionary entry to find the right meaning and the right way to say a word.

Peri significa «alrededor» y meter significa «medir», así que el perímetro es la medida alrededor de una figura. Dia significa «a través», así que el diámetro es la medida que cruza un círculo. Casi todas las palabras de tus libros de matemáticas y de ciencias se formaron con partes griegas y latinas. Con Lumi y Nova vas a aprender raíces como geo, gon, poly, sect, frac, equ, iso, therm, chron, tele y micro, descubrir palabras impostoras que solo se parecen a una raíz, ver cómo un sufijo cambia la clase de palabra, construir palabras con sus partes, comprobar una palabra nueva en su oración y leer una entrada del diccionario para encontrar el significado y la pronunciación correctos.

Part 1 · Figure out a new word

“On our field trip to Yellowstone National Park, the ranger pointed to a pool of steaming water. ‘These hot springs are geothermal,’ she said. ‘The water is heated by hot rock deep inside the earth, not by the sun.’”

What does geothermal probably mean? Explain how the two roots, geo and therm, and the words in the passage helped you. Then tell how you would check your guess.

¿Qué significa probablemente geothermal? Explica cómo te ayudaron las dos raíces, geo y therm, y las palabras del texto. Después di cómo comprobarías tu respuesta.

Sentence starters: Geothermal probably means ___. The root geo means ___, and therm means ___. The ranger says that ___, and that shows ___. To check my guess, I would ___.

Useful words: root, prefix, suffix, context clue, confirm, dictionary, entry, part of speech.

Challenge: also say what part of speech geothermal is in the passage and how you know. Then write two more words with the root therm and two more with geo, and explain what each one means.

Reto: di también qué clase de palabra es geothermal en el texto y cómo lo sabes. Después escribe otras dos palabras con la raíz therm y otras dos con geo, y explica qué significa cada una.

Part 2 · Same spelling, two ways to say it

The dictionary shows two ways to say the word estimate: EST-uh-mit (a noun: a careful guess) and EST-uh-mayt (a verb: to make a careful guess). Write one math sentence with each one. Then explain how a reader can tell which one you mean.

El diccionario muestra dos maneras de pronunciar la palabra estimate: EST-uh-mit (sustantivo: un cálculo aproximado) y EST-uh-mayt (verbo: calcular de forma aproximada). Escribe una oración de matemáticas con cada una. Después explica cómo sabe el lector cuál de las dos quieres decir.

Sentence starters: In my first sentence, estimate is a noun because ___. In my second sentence, it is a verb because ___. A reader can tell by ___.

Challenge: do the same with another word from the game: approximate, progress, contrast, increase or survey.

Reto: haz lo mismo con otra palabra del juego: approximate, progress, contrast, increase o survey.

Part 1. Geothermal probably means heated by the earth. The root geo means earth, and therm means heat. The ranger confirms it: “The water is heated by hot rock deep inside the earth, not by the sun.” To check my guess, I would look up geothermal in a dictionary and read the meaning that fits hot springs.

Challenge. Geothermal is an adjective: it describes the hot springs, after “These hot springs are.” Other therm words: thermometer (an instrument that measures heat) and thermal (keeping in heat). Other geo words: geology (the study of the earth) and geography (the study of the earth's places and maps).

Part 2. “My estimate for the length of the classroom is 9 meters.” (EST-uh-mit, a noun: it comes after “My” and names a thing.) “Estimate the answer before you multiply.” (EST-uh-mayt, a verb: it is an instruction that tells you what to do.) A reader can tell by the job the word does in the sentence: a noun names a thing, and a verb is an action.

Your words can be different. What matters is that you used the roots, quoted the passage and explained how you would check the meaning.
