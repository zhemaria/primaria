Red rods and “absurd” numbers

About 2,000 years ago, Chinese scholars finished a book called The Nine Chapters on the Mathematical Art. It has 246 problems about fields, trade, taxes and building. They did the calculations with small rods on a counting board. The eighth chapter has rules for working with positive and negative numbers.

In the year 263, the mathematician Liu Hui explained them: there are two kinds of rods, one for gains and one for losses, and they are called positive and negative. Red rods were positive and black rods were negative. The money from a sale was positive; the money spent on a purchase was negative.

In Europe the story was very different. In the third century, the Greek mathematician Diophantus called an equation like 4x + 20 = 0 absurd, because its answer would be negative. More than a thousand years later, in the 1500s, the German mathematician Michael Stifel still called negative numbers numeri absurdi: “absurd numbers.”

Nova: Absurd? My phone says it is −5 °F in Chicago today!

Lumi: You use every day an idea that took Europe centuries to accept. And look: one red rod and one black rod together make zero. A gain of 1 and a loss of 1 cancel.

Nova: A zero pair! So 5 red rods and 3 black rods make 3 zero pairs, and 2 red rods are left: 5 + (−3) = 2.

Now it is your turn to calculate like the scholars of the Nine Chapters.

Hace unos 2000 años, unos sabios chinos terminaron un libro llamado Los nueve capítulos sobre el arte matemático, con 246 problemas sobre campos, comercio, impuestos y construcción. Calculaban con varillas pequeñas sobre un tablero, y el octavo capítulo tiene reglas para trabajar con números positivos y negativos. En el año 263, el matemático Liu Hui las explicó: hay dos clases de varillas, una para las ganancias y otra para las pérdidas; las rojas eran positivas y las negras, negativas. En Europa fue muy distinto: en el siglo III, el griego Diofanto llamó absurda una ecuación como 4x + 20 = 0, porque su respuesta sería negativa, y en el siglo XVI el alemán Michael Stifel todavía llamaba a los negativos numeri absurdi, «números absurdos». Nova se ríe: su teléfono marca −5 °F en Chicago. Lumi le muestra que una varilla roja y una negra juntas hacen cero: un par cero.

Liu Hui also wrote that the two kinds of rods could be told apart by their position: upright for positive and slanted for negative. In these games the red rods stand up and the black rods lean, so you can tell them apart even without the colors.

Final task: explain it yourself

Part 1 · Explain

Elías says: “−8 − (−3) = −11, because when you have two negatives you put them together.” Is Elías right? What is −8 − (−3)?

Elías dice: «−8 − (−3) = −11, porque cuando hay dos negativos se juntan». ¿Tiene razón Elías? ¿Cuánto es −8 − (−3)?

Sentence starters: Elías is ___ because ___. With rods: I put 8 black rods on the board and take away ___ black rods, so ___ are left. Or: −8 − (−3) = −8 + ___ = ___.

Useful words: opposite, zero pair, take away, add the opposite, number line, gain, loss.

Challenge: explain also 3 − (−5) with rods. The board has only 3 red rods, and you must take away 5 black rods. What do you add first, and why does it not change the value?

Reto: explica también 3 − (−5) con varillas. El tablero solo tiene 3 varillas rojas y hay que quitar 5 negras. ¿Qué agregas primero y por qué eso no cambia el valor?

Part 2 · Apply the idea to a new problem

On a winter morning in Chicago, the temperature at 6 a.m. is −7 °F. By noon it rises 12 degrees, and by night it falls 9 degrees. Athena says the night temperature is 4 °F. Is she right? Write an expression, find the temperature and explain. Then write your own problem like this one.

Una mañana de invierno en Chicago, la temperatura a las 6 a. m. es −7 °F. Para el mediodía sube 12 grados y por la noche baja 9 grados. Athena dice que la temperatura de la noche es 4 °F. ¿Tiene razón? Escribe una expresión, halla la temperatura y explica. Después escribe tu propio problema parecido.

Sentence starters: Athena is ___ because ___. My expression is −7 + ___ − ___. At noon it is ___ °F, and at night it is ___ °F.

Challenge: during the next 4 hours the temperature falls 1.5 degrees every hour. Write the change as a product with a negative number, and find the final temperature.

Reto: durante las 4 horas siguientes la temperatura baja 1.5 grados cada hora. Escribe el cambio como un producto con un número negativo y halla la temperatura final.

Part 1. Elías is not right. Subtracting −3 means taking away a loss of 3. With rods: I put 8 black rods on the board and take away 3 black rods; 5 black rods are left, so the answer is −5. With the rule: −8 − (−3) = −8 + 3 = −5. On the number line, I start at −8 and move 3 to the right, to −5.

Challenge. 3 − (−5) = 8. The board has 3 red rods and no black rods to take away, so first I add 5 zero pairs (5 red and 5 black). A zero pair is worth 0, so the value is still 3. Now I take away the 5 black rods, and 3 + 5 = 8 red rods are left.

Part 2. Athena is not right. The expression is −7 + 12 − 9. At noon: −7 + 12 = 5 °F. At night: 5 − 9 = −4 °F. Athena forgot that 5 − 9 goes below zero: the night temperature is −4 °F.

Challenge. The change is 4 × (−1.5) = −6 degrees, so the final temperature is −4 + (−6) = −10 °F.
