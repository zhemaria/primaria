For the grown-up

How to use the series

The modules are ordered by what depends on what, not by difficulty within a single session. A child may spend weeks on the first one before touching the second, and that is what you should expect. Sessions of five to eight minutes are enough.

All three modules read their instructions out loud, have an easy mode, an animated demonstration, and the option to mark an exercise as done on paper, so no child gets stuck for lack of motor control or for not reading yet.

What each one practices

  • Tracing paths: stroke control and direction, top to bottom and left to right. Standard 5.A, writing tools and letterlike forms.
  • Capital letters: letter formation, stroke order, and writing their own first name. Standards 4.B and 5.A.
  • Knowing the letters: matching capital with lowercase, alphabet order, and the first sound in a word. Standards 4.A, 4.B and 4.C.

Standards

The codes come from the Illinois Early Learning and Development Standards for preschool, language arts: 4.A print features, 4.B alphabet knowledge, 4.C spoken words and sounds, 5.A interest and ability in writing. Four and five year olds are not asked for handwriting, they are asked to recognize letters and know where each one starts.

In the classroom

Keep the tablet flat on the table, never held in the hand. Alternate the screen with paper and crayon: a screen offers no resistance, and resistance is part of what strengthens the hand.
