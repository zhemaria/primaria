      For the grown-up

      Tracing their own name

      Type the child's name here and the activity builds a sequence from its letters. This is the most meaningful task at this age, because the letter stops being a drawing and becomes theirs.

      Settings

      In easy mode the margin of error is wider and leaving the path does not interrupt the stroke.

      Why this order

      The letters are grouped by motor difficulty, not alphabetically. Straight lines first, which only ask the hand to go down and across. Then the slanted ones, which require control of the angle. Curves last, because they are the last to be mastered.

      The Illinois Early Learning and Development Standards do not ask for perfect handwriting at four and five: they ask for recognition and directionality. That is why the activity blocks a backwards stroke but forgives a shaky one.

      How to help

      Say the letter name and its sound while the child traces: this is M, mmm, like moon. Linking the movement to the sound is what later supports reading.
