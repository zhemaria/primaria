The river that runs backward

In the 1800s, Chicago grew faster than almost any city in the world. Its sewers emptied into the Chicago River, and the river flowed into Lake Michigan, the same lake where the city took its drinking water. Outbreaks of diseases such as cholera and typhoid made people sick, and the city needed a solution.

A canal across the divide. West of Chicago there is a very low ridge that separates the waters that go to the Great Lakes from the waters that go to the Mississippi. In 1892 workers began digging the Sanitary and Ship Canal, 28 miles long, from the South Branch of the river at Robey Street (today Damen Avenue) to Lockport. At times thousands of people worked on it at once, with dynamite and machines invented for the job. The canal was dug deep enough that gravity would pull the water inland, away from the lake.

A secret morning. The city of St. Louis, on the Mississippi, was preparing a lawsuit to stop the project. So on the cold morning of January 2, 1900, with almost no announcement, the trustees of the Sanitary District cut the last earthen dam and let the water into the canal. On January 17 the works at Lockport were opened, and the river was running backward. Missouri took the case to the U.S. Supreme Court, which decided in favor of Illinois on February 19, 1906.

Nova: How can a river run backward? Nobody pushes it!

Lumi: Gravity does. Water flows downhill, so the engineers made the way to the Mississippi a little lower than the lake.

Nova: So it is all about the slope.

Lumi: Exactly: how much the height changes for each step forward. In math, slope is rise over run. Let’s measure some!

Today the canal still carries water and barges, and the river still runs away from the lake. In these games you will use slope and linear equations, the same tools engineers use to plan canals, roads and ramps.

En el siglo XIX, Chicago creció más rápido que casi cualquier ciudad del mundo. Sus alcantarillas desembocaban en el río Chicago, y el río corría hacia el lago Michigan, el mismo lago del que la ciudad tomaba el agua potable. Los brotes de enfermedades como el cólera y la fiebre tifoidea enfermaban a la gente, y la ciudad necesitaba una solución. Al oeste de Chicago hay una divisoria muy baja que separa las aguas que van a los Grandes Lagos de las que van al Misisipi. En 1892 empezaron a cavar el Canal Sanitario y de Navegación, de 28 millas, desde el brazo sur del río en la calle Robey (hoy avenida Damen) hasta Lockport, a veces con miles de trabajadores a la vez, dinamita y máquinas inventadas para la obra. El canal se cavó lo bastante hondo para que la gravedad llevara el agua tierra adentro, lejos del lago. La ciudad de St. Louis, sobre el Misisipi, preparaba una demanda para detener el proyecto; por eso, la fría mañana del 2 de enero de 1900, casi sin aviso, los administradores del Distrito Sanitario cortaron la última presa de tierra y dejaron entrar el agua en el canal. El 17 de enero se abrieron las compuertas de Lockport y el río ya corría al revés. Misuri llevó el caso a la Corte Suprema, que falló a favor de Illinois el 19 de febrero de 1906. El secreto fue la pendiente: el agua corre cuesta abajo.

About 40 million cubic yards of earth and rock were removed to build the canal. Between 1903 and 1907 it was extended to Joliet, where a lock and a powerhouse use a drop of 34 feet. You can read more in the Encyclopedia of Chicago and on the website of the Metropolitan Water Reclamation District, the agency that still runs the canal.

Part 1. Elías is not right. Slope is rise over run, not run over rise. From (1, 2) to (4, 8) the rise is 8 − 2 = 6 and the run is 4 − 1 = 3, so the slope is 6/3 = 2. The line goes up 2 for every 1 step to the right. A slope of 1/2 would be a much less steep line.

Challenge. The small triangle has rise 2 and run 1: 2/1 = 2. The big triangle has rise 6 and run 3: 6/3 = 2. The two triangles are similar (same angles, sides in the same ratio), so the ratio rise/run is the same anywhere on a straight line.

Part 2. Salma did not multiply the 2 by 3. The correct first step is 3x − 6 = 2x + 5. Subtract 2x from both sides: x − 6 = 5. Add 6: x = 11. Check: 3(11 − 2) = 3 × 9 = 27 and 2(11) + 5 = 27. Both sides are equal, so x = 11 is correct.

Challenge. Plan A: y = 5x + 25. Plan B: y = 10x. They cost the same when 5x + 25 = 10x, so 25 = 5x and x = 5 classes (both cost $50). With 8 classes, Plan A costs 5(8) + 25 = $65 and Plan B costs 10(8) = $80, so Plan A is better.
