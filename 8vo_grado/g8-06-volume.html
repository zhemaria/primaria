Archimedes and his tomb

Archimedes lived about 2,300 years ago in Syracuse, a Greek city on the island of Sicily. He invented machines, studied levers and floating objects, and wrote some of the greatest mathematics of the ancient world.

His favorite discovery. In his book On the Sphere and Cylinder, Archimedes proved that a sphere has exactly two thirds of the volume of the cylinder that fits around it, the cylinder with the same radius and a height equal to the diameter. With that, he found for the first time an exact way to measure the volume of a sphere. A cone with the same base and the same height has one third of the cylinder. So the cone, the sphere and the cylinder are in the ratio 1 : 2 : 3.

The tomb. According to Plutarch, Archimedes asked his family and friends to place on his grave a cylinder enclosing a sphere, with the ratio written on it. He died in 212 BCE, when Roman soldiers captured Syracuse. More than a hundred years later, in 75 BCE, the Roman writer Cicero was working in Sicily and went looking for the tomb. He found it near one of the city gates, hidden by thorny bushes: a small column with a sphere and a cylinder on top.

Arquímedes vivió hace unos 2300 años en Siracusa, una ciudad griega de la isla de Sicilia. Inventó máquinas, estudió las palancas y los cuerpos que flotan, y escribió algunas de las matemáticas más grandes del mundo antiguo. En su libro Sobre la esfera y el cilindro demostró que una esfera tiene exactamente dos tercios del volumen del cilindro que la envuelve (el de igual radio y altura igual al diámetro); así encontró por primera vez una manera exacta de medir el volumen de una esfera. Un cono de la misma base y la misma altura tiene un tercio del cilindro: el cono, la esfera y el cilindro están en la razón 1 : 2 : 3. Según Plutarco, Arquímedes pidió a su familia y amigos que pusieran sobre su tumba un cilindro que encerrara una esfera, con la razón escrita. Murió en el 212 a. C., cuando los soldados romanos tomaron Siracusa. Más de cien años después, en el 75 a. C., el escritor romano Cicerón trabajaba en Sicilia y fue a buscar la tumba: la encontró cerca de una de las puertas de la ciudad, escondida entre zarzas: una pequeña columna con una esfera y un cilindro encima.

Cicero tells this story himself in his book Tusculan Disputations (Book V). Archimedes also proved that the surface area of the sphere is two thirds of the surface of the cylinder, including its two circular ends.

Part 1. Sophia is not right. The cylinder has V = π × 3² × 4 = 36π ≈ 113.04 cm³. The cone has V = ⅓ × π × 3² × 4 = 12π ≈ 37.68 cm³. The cone has the same base and height, but it gets narrower up to a point, so it holds only one third of the cylinder: three cones of water fill the cylinder.

Challenge. Sphere: V = ⁴⁄₃ × π × 3³ = ⁴⁄₃ × 27π = 36π. Cylinder: V = π × 3² × 6 = 54π. And 36π ÷ 54π = 36/54 = 2/3, exactly as Archimedes proved.

Part 2. Salma is not right: 12 inches across is the diameter, and the formula uses the radius, which is half: 6 inches. V = 3.14 × 6² × 2 = 3.14 × 36 × 2 = 226.08 cubic inches. Using the diameter made her answer 4 times too big, because 12² is 4 times 6².

Challenge. Cylinder: π × 10² × 40 = 4,000π. Half a sphere: ½ × ⁴⁄₃ × π × 10³ = ²⁄₃ × 1,000π ≈ 666.7π. Total ≈ 4,666.7π ≈ 14,653.3 cubic feet.

The module opens with a true story from the history of mathematics: Archimedes proved in On the Sphere and Cylinder that a sphere has two thirds of the volume of the cylinder around it, and asked for that figure on his tomb, which Cicero found in Syracuse in 75 BCE. At the Easy level the answers use π ≈ 3.14 and the radius is given; at the Challenge level some answers are written in terms of π (as on the Illinois Assessment of Readiness), the diameter is given instead of the radius, a missing radius or height must be found, and the solids are combined. The most common mistakes, using the diameter as the radius, forgetting the ⅓ of the cone, using r² instead of r³ in the sphere and multiplying by 2 instead of squaring, get their own hints, and a wrong answer always brings a hint, never the answer. The optional “Ayuda en español” button translates only the instruction or the question.
