Thales and the shadow of the pyramid

Thales of Miletus lived more than 2,500 years ago in what is now Turkey. The Greeks counted him among the Seven Sages. He traveled to Egypt, where the great pyramids were already about 2,000 years old, and, according to the ancient writers, he measured their height without climbing them.

Two versions of the story. The oldest version comes from Hieronymus of Rhodes, a student of Aristotle, quoted by the biographer Diogenes Laertius: Thales waited for the moment of the day when our shadows are as long as we are tall. At that moment, the shadow of the pyramid was also as long as the pyramid was tall, so he only had to measure the shadow. A later version, told by Plutarch, is even cleverer: Thales put up a stick at the end of the pyramid’s shadow, and the sun’s rays made two triangles. The pyramid was to the stick as the shadow of the pyramid was to the shadow of the stick.

Why it works. At the same moment, the sun’s rays hit the pyramid and the stick at the same angle. So the two triangles have the same angles, and triangles with the same angles are similar: one is a bigger copy of the other. In similar figures, all the lengths grow by the same scale factor.

Nova: So if the stick’s shadow is twice as long as the stick, the pyramid’s shadow is twice as long as the pyramid?

Lumi: Exactly. The same shape, a different size. That is a dilation.

Nova: We could measure the Willis Tower that way!

Lumi: Yes, if we could see the end of its shadow. Today we will move, flip, turn and grow figures, and see what stays the same.

We do not have any writing by Thales himself: all we know comes from writers who lived centuries later. But the idea in the story is exactly right, and it is one of the most useful ideas in geometry.

Tales de Mileto vivió hace más de 2500 años en lo que hoy es Turquía. Los griegos lo contaban entre los Siete Sabios. Viajó a Egipto, donde las grandes pirámides ya tenían unos 2000 años y, según los escritores antiguos, midió su altura sin subirse a ellas. La versión más antigua viene de Jerónimo de Rodas, alumno de Aristóteles, citado por el biógrafo Diógenes Laercio: Tales esperó el momento del día en que nuestras sombras miden lo mismo que nuestra estatura; en ese momento, la sombra de la pirámide también medía lo mismo que su altura, así que solo tuvo que medir la sombra. Una versión posterior, de Plutarco, es todavía más ingeniosa: Tales clavó un palo al final de la sombra de la pirámide, y los rayos del sol formaron dos triángulos; la pirámide era al palo como la sombra de la pirámide era a la sombra del palo. Funciona porque, en el mismo momento, los rayos del sol llegan con el mismo ángulo; los dos triángulos tienen los mismos ángulos y son semejantes: uno es una copia más grande del otro. No conservamos nada escrito por el propio Tales: todo lo que sabemos viene de autores que vivieron siglos después.

Long before Thales, Egyptian scribes already described the slope of a pyramid with a number called the seked; Ahmes, the scribe of the Rhind papyrus, solved problems with it. The Great Pyramid of Giza was about 146 meters (481 feet) tall when it was built.

Part 1. Israel is not right: in similar triangles the lengths are multiplied by the same scale factor, not increased by the same amount. The sun’s rays hit Selena and the tree at the same angle, so the two triangles are similar. The tree’s shadow is 20 ÷ 4 = 5 times as long as Selena’s shadow, so the tree is 5 times as tall as Selena: 5 × 5 = 25 feet. Check: 25 ÷ 20 = 5 ÷ 4 = 1.25.

Challenge. Shadow ÷ height = 3 ÷ 2 = 1.5 for everything at that moment. The tower’s shadow is 1,450 × 1.5 = 2,175 feet.

Part 2. Daniel is not right: he reflected over the y-axis, because he changed the sign of x. Reflecting over the x-axis keeps x and changes the sign of y: A′(1, −2), B′(4, −2), C′(1, −5). A reflection keeps all lengths and angles the same, so the two triangles are congruent.

Challenge. Multiply every coordinate by 2: A″(2, 4), B″(8, 4), C″(2, 10). The new triangle has the same angles but sides twice as long, so it is similar but not congruent. AB measured 3, so its image measures 6.
