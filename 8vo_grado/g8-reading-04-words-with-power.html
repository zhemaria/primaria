Words with power

Why was the right angle never cold? Because it was always 90 degrees. Words can carry two meanings, say the opposite of what they mean, or turn a stubborn person into a determined one. With Lumi and Nova you will find meanings in context, take apart Greek and Latin roots, catch verbal irony and puns, solve analogies, feel the connotation of a word, and use a dictionary to choose the right meaning and pronunciation.

¿Por qué el ángulo recto nunca tenía frío? Porque siempre estaba a 90 grados. Las palabras pueden tener dos significados, decir lo contrario de lo que quieren decir o convertir a una persona terca en una persona decidida. Con Lumi y Nova vas a descubrir significados por el contexto, desarmar raíces griegas y latinas, reconocer la ironía verbal y los juegos de palabras, resolver analogías, sentir la connotación de una palabra y usar el diccionario para elegir el significado y la pronunciación correctos.

The speech: Leo at the Science Fair

1Good morning, judges. My project is about a drone that went on a very short vacation: to the bottom of Lake Michigan.

2I followed my father's first rule, but I ignored the second one, and the wind was merciless.

3Using the Pythagorean theorem, I found that the drone was 500 feet away from me, which exceeded the distance its battery could fly back against the wind.

4Some people would call my mistake foolish. I prefer to call it educational.

5My new drone, Daedalus, is meticulous about safety: it turns back automatically when the battery drops to 30 percent.

6I guess you could say that my first drone and I had a falling out.

Part 1 · Find the power words

Find and explain four things in Leo's speech: one example of verbal irony, one pun, one word whose Greek or Latin root helps you understand it, and one word chosen for its connotation. Quote each one and explain how it works.

Busca y explica cuatro cosas en el discurso de Leo: un ejemplo de ironía verbal, un juego de palabras, una palabra cuya raíz griega o latina te ayuda a entenderla y una palabra elegida por su connotación. Cita cada una y explica cómo funciona.

Sentence starters: “___” is verbal irony, because Leo says ___ but means ___. “___” is a pun, because ___ can mean ___ and ___. The word “___” has the root ___, which means ___, so ___. Leo chose “___” instead of “___” because ___.

Useful words: verbal irony, pun, root, prefix, suffix, connotation, positive, negative, context clue, literal, figurative.

Challenge: use context clues to explain what meticulous means in paragraph 5. Then check your guess in a dictionary and say whether you were right.

Reto: usa las pistas del contexto para explicar qué significa meticulous en el párrafo 5. Después comprueba tu suposición en un diccionario y di si acertaste.

Part 2 · Use the power of words

Describe the same moment twice, in one or two sentences each: Sami posting a fake message in the class chat. First, choose words with a positive or playful connotation. Then choose words with a negative connotation. Underline or name the words that change the feeling.

Describe dos veces el mismo momento, en una o dos oraciones cada vez: Sami publica un mensaje falso en el chat de la clase. Primero, elige palabras con una connotación positiva o juguetona. Después, palabras con una connotación negativa. Subraya o nombra las palabras que cambian el sentimiento.

Word bank: joke, prank, trick, lie, playful, mischievous, dishonest, harmless, cruel, giggled, sneered, posted, spread.

Challenge: write your own pun or your own example of verbal irony about math or science, and explain why it works.

Part 1. “A drone that went on a very short vacation: to the bottom of Lake Michigan” is verbal irony, because a vacation sounds fun, but Leo means that the drone was lost in the lake. “My first drone and I had a falling out” is a pun, because a falling out means an argument, and the drone also literally fell out of the sky. The word “exceeded” has the prefix ex-, beyond, and the root ceed, go, so it means the distance went beyond what the battery could do. Leo chose “educational” instead of “foolish” because educational has a positive connotation: it turns his mistake into a lesson.

Challenge. The drone turns back automatically when the battery drops to 30 percent, so meticulous must mean very careful about every detail. The dictionary says “showing great care and attention to detail,” so my guess was right.

Part 2. Positive: “Sami posted a playful joke in the class chat, and twenty friends giggled at once.” Negative: “Sami spread a dishonest message in the class chat and sneered as twenty classmates panicked.” The words playful, joke and giggled make it sound harmless; dishonest, spread and sneered make it sound cruel.

Challenge. “I tried to write a joke about the number zero, but it was pointless.” It is a pun, because pointless means without a purpose, and zero is a number with no value.

Your words can be different. What matters is that you can explain how each word does its job.

The sentences connect with the eighth grade math stories (Thales, Galileo, Archimedes, Hubble, Fisher, the Chicago canal) and with the stories of the reading modules (Leo, Maya, Sami). At the Easy level there are fewer options; at the Challenge level a second step asks for the strongest context clue, a word from the same root family, the true meaning of an ironic line, the relationship in an analogy or the pronunciation. Every wrong answer gets a hint, never the answer. The final task asks the student to find verbal irony, a pun, a root and a connotation in a short speech, and to describe one event twice with words that change its feeling.
