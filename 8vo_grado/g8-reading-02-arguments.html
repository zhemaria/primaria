When two sides disagree, who has the evidence?

Daniel wants school to start later. Sophia wants a garden on an empty lot. Israel wants every class to visit a science museum. Each one knows that some people disagree, and each one answers them. Final task: explain it yourself

There are no options to choose from this time. Judge an argument like an expert: is the evidence relevant? Does the author really answer the other side? Then write your own argument with a fair rebuttal.

Esta vez no hay opciones para elegir. Juzga un argumento como un experto: ¿la evidencia es relevante? ¿Responde de verdad el autor al otro lado? Después escribe tu propio argumento con una refutación justa.

The essay: Every Eighth Grader Should Learn to Code, by Mateo

1Our school teaches art, music and Spanish, but not one class teaches how to write a computer program. I believe every eighth grader at our school should take a coding class.

2First, coding helps students in math. When our after-school club wrote programs to draw shapes, members had to use coordinates, angles and slope. Our math teacher noticed that 9 of the 12 club members raised their grades on the next geometry test. The first computer mouse was made of wood.

3Second, coding opens doors to jobs. Many jobs today, from medicine to farming, use computer programs, and people who can write them are in demand.

4Some people say that the school day is already full, and a new class would take time away from reading and math. They are just afraid of computers.

5Everyone who learns to code will become rich. Let's give every student the chance to speak the language of computers!

Part 1 · Judge the argument

What is Mateo's claim? Find one piece of relevant evidence, one detail that is irrelevant, and one claim that is not supported. Quote all three and explain how you know. Then look at paragraph 4: does Mateo really answer the other side?

¿Cuál es la afirmación de Mateo? Busca una evidencia relevante, un detalle que no es relevante y una afirmación sin apoyo. Cita las tres y explica cómo lo sabes. Después mira el párrafo 4: ¿responde Mateo de verdad al otro lado?

Sentence starters: Mateo's claim is ___. The evidence “___” is relevant, because ___. The detail “___” is irrelevant, because ___. The claim “___” is not supported, because ___. In paragraph 4, Mateo ___, so his rebuttal is ___.

Useful words: claim, reason, evidence, relevant, irrelevant, sufficient, counterclaim, rebuttal, sound reasoning, overgeneralization.

Challenge: is the evidence for Mateo's first reason sufficient? Explain what makes it weak and what evidence would make it stronger.

Reto: ¿es suficiente la evidencia de la primera razón de Mateo? Explica qué la hace débil y qué evidencia la haría más fuerte.

Part 2 · Rewrite the rebuttal, then argue your own side

First, rewrite paragraph 4 so that Mateo describes the other side fairly and answers it with a reason or a solution. Then write a short argument (six or seven sentences) about a change you would like at your school or in your neighborhood. Include a claim, two reasons, relevant evidence, a counterclaim and a respectful rebuttal.

Primero, reescribe el párrafo 4 para que Mateo describa al otro lado con justicia y le responda con una razón o una solución. Después escribe un argumento corto (seis o siete oraciones) sobre un cambio que te gustaría en tu escuela o en tu barrio. Incluye una afirmación, dos razones, evidencia relevante, un contraargumento y una refutación respetuosa.

Sentence starters: Some people say ___. That is a fair concern, because ___. However, ___. · I believe ___. The first reason is ___. For example, ___. The second reason is ___. In fact, ___. Some people believe ___. That is true, but ___.

Challenge: imagine a second text that disagrees with your evidence. Where would the two texts disagree: on a fact or on an interpretation? How could a reader check?

Reto: imagina un segundo texto que no está de acuerdo con tu evidencia. ¿En qué no estarían de acuerdo: en un hecho o en una interpretación? ¿Cómo podría comprobarlo un lector?

Part 1. Mateo’s claim is that every eighth grader at his school should take a coding class. The evidence “members had to use coordinates, angles and slope” is relevant, because it shows how coding connects to math. The detail “The first computer mouse was made of wood” is irrelevant: it may be an interesting fact, but it says nothing about math or jobs. The claim “Everyone who learns to code will become rich” is not supported; it is an overgeneralization with no evidence. In paragraph 4, Mateo describes the other side, but instead of answering their concern about time, he attacks them (“They are just afraid of computers”), so it is not a real rebuttal.

Challenge. The evidence for the first reason is weak because it comes from only 12 club members, and those students chose to join a club, so they may have been interested in math already. Grades from a whole class that took coding, compared with a class that did not, would make it stronger.

Part 2. New paragraph 4: “Some people say that the school day is already full, and a new class would take time away from reading and math. That is a fair concern, because those subjects are important. However, a coding class could replace one study hall a week, and its projects could use the math we already learn.”

Own argument: “I believe our park should have more lights along the walking path. The first reason is safety. For example, our block club counted three broken lights on the path last month. The second reason is that more people would use the park in the evening. In fact, 30 of the 40 neighbors we asked said they would walk there after dark if it were brighter. Some people believe lights cost too much. That is true, but solar lights cost less to run, and the park district has a fund for repairs.”

Challenge. Another text might say that 30 of 40 neighbors is too small a survey. We would disagree on an interpretation, not on the fact. A reader could check by asking more neighbors or by finding a study from another park.

Your words can be different. What matters is that you can judge whether evidence is relevant and sufficient, and that your argument answers the other side with respect.

Aligned to the Illinois eighth grade English Language Arts standards RI.8.6 (determine an author's point of view or purpose in a text and analyze how the author acknowledges and responds to conflicting evidence or viewpoints), RI.8.8 (delineate and evaluate the argument and specific claims in a text, assessing whether the reasoning is sound and the evidence is relevant and sufficient; recognize when irrelevant evidence is introduced) and RI.8.9 (analyze a case in which two or more texts provide conflicting information on the same topic and identify where the texts disagree on matters of fact or interpretation). The three essays are written by eighth graders and include reasons, evidence, a counterclaim with a rebuttal, one piece of irrelevant evidence and one unsupported claim on purpose. The three pairs connect with the eighth grade math stories and show real disagreements between sources: Hieronymus and Plutarch on how Thales measured a pyramid, Viviani and today's historians on Galileo and the Tower of Pisa, and later retellings and Fisher's own book on the lady tasting tea. The questions ask where the texts agree, where they disagree and how a reader could check. At the Easy level there are fewer options and the sentence being studied is highlighted; at the Challenge level the student must prove the answer and tell apart all seven jobs a sentence can have. Every wrong answer gets a hint, never the answer. The final task asks the student to judge an essay whose rebuttal is really an insult, to rewrite it fairly and to write an argument with a respectful rebuttal. The optional “Ayuda en español” button translates only the instruction or the question, never the texts or the answers, so the reading stays in English. Each game has its own direct link: #proposito, #afirmacion, #tipo, #apoyo, #evaluar, #comparar and #tarea.

Alineado con los estándares de Artes del Lenguaje en Inglés de Illinois para octavo grado: RI.8.6 (determinar el punto de vista del autor y analizar cómo reconoce y responde a evidencias o puntos de vista en conflicto), RI.8.8 (seguir y evaluar el argumento, juzgando si el razonamiento es sólido y si la evidencia es relevante y suficiente, y reconocer la evidencia irrelevante) y RI.8.9 (analizar dos textos que dan información en conflicto sobre el mismo tema e identificar si no están de acuerdo en un hecho o en una interpretación). Ante un error, siempre una pista, nunca la respuesta. Los juegos son en inglés; el botón «Ayuda en español» traduce solo la consigna o la pregunta.
